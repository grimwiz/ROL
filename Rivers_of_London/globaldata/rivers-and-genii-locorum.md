# The Rivers & the Genii Locorum

<!-- Static world reference. Original, spoiler-free summary of widely-known setting facts; no plot, no quoted text. Player-safe. -->

Reference for the river spirits and other *genii locorum* — the kind of thing a
reader of the books takes for granted, so the AI can field player questions
about them. Setting facts only: no case plot, no character arcs.

## What a genius loci is

A *genius loci* (plural *genii locorum*) is the spirit, or presiding
personality, of a place — most famously a river, but also springs, hills,
woods, and even a stretch of town. They are powers of the demi-monde rather than
ordinary ghosts: they embody their place, draw strength from it, and tend to be
strongest on their own ground. People who take on the role can be very long-lived
and carry real, if local, power.

## The Thames, divided

London's river is not held by a single power. By long custom the Thames is split
in two:

- **Upriver — Father Thames, the "Old Man".** The ancient power of the
  non-tidal, rural upper river and its country tributaries.
- **Downriver — Mama Thames.** The power of the tidal river through London, who
  took up the city's stretch in the modern era, with a court of tributary
  spirits across the capital.

The two sides keep an uneasy peace, brokered and maintained with the help of the
Folly. The boundary between their reaches is itself a matter of negotiation.

## Courts and tributaries

Each side has a "family" — lesser genii locorum attached to the Thames's
tributaries and associated waters, treated as that power's children or court.
The downriver tributaries in particular are personified as a set of distinct
London river-spirits, each tied to a named (and often now buried or culverted)
stream. They have their own territories, tempers, and rivalries.

## Dealing with them (for investigators)

- **They are powers, not suspects.** The Folly approaches them with courtesy and
  through standing agreements, not warrants. Nightingale brokers; he does not
  command them.
- **Hospitality and gifts matter.** Demi-monde etiquette runs on obligation —
  accepting food, drink, or a favour can carry real weight. Bargains are taken
  seriously on both sides.
- **Home ground.** A genius loci is strongest in and around its own water;
  pushing one on its own territory is unwise.
- **Cold iron** unsettles and can harm many demi-monde beings; it is a
  consideration in any tense encounter (see `magic-overview.md` / the demi-monde
  rules).
- **Luck and favour.** Being granted a river's favour is a tangible boon in this
  world; offending one is a tangible problem.

## Beyond London

Other rivers and places across Britain have their own genii locorum, with their
own histories and dispositions; London's arrangement is not the only model. A
local power encountered outside the city should be read on its own terms.
