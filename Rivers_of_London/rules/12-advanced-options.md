# Advanced Options

<!-- Source: additional-rules, printed pages 309-317 and 321-335. Status: extracted-draft. -->

This file collects optional and advanced rules. Use them selectively. Standard investigator creation, skills, combat, damage, and magic remain in the earlier numbered files.

Chapter 9's lower-fae and Quiet Person investigator rules are extracted in `11-demi-monde.md`.

## Advanced Character Creation

These options require GM permission.

### Free Characteristic Allocation

Instead of assigning the standard `280` characteristic points in blocks of 10, players may assign the same `280` points in any values they choose.

Rules:

- The investigator still has five characteristics.
- When calculating half characteristic values, round down.
- The standard characteristic range is `30` to `80`.
- If the GM wants more exceptional investigators, they may widen the allowed range to `20` to `90`.

### Age and MOV

Use this optional table if age should have a mechanical effect. It is intended for mundane human characters; magically affected characters may be exempt.

| Age | MOV adjustment |
| --- | ---: |
| 40s | `-1` |
| 50s | `-2` |
| 60s | `-3` |
| 70s | `-4` |
| 80s | `-5` |

Apply the adjustment after calculating the investigator's normal MOV.

### Experience Packages

Experience packages create more experienced starting investigators. Each package grants extra competence and requires the investigator to take one disadvantage.

Unless a package says otherwise:

- Use the listed base occupation.
- Gain `60` additional skill points.
- Spend those points on required and recommended occupation skills.
- No skill may be raised above `80%` by the package.
- Choose one disadvantage from this file.

| Package | Base occupation | Extra rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Academic | Lecturer | Standard package rules. |
| Criminal | Criminal | GM should check campaign fit, especially in a police or law-enforcement group. |
| Ex-Military | Service Member | Standard package rules. |
| Paranormal | Parapsychologist, or another occupation with GM permission | The investigator must have a backstory reason for knowing about the demi-monde. |
| Police | Police Officer/Detective | Standard package rules. |
| Experienced Investigator | Any standard occupation | Gain `60` skill points. Either take one additional new skill at `60%`, or distribute the points among existing skills. No skill may exceed `80%` by this method. |
| Spellcaster | Any standard occupation | Requires the Magical advantage. Magic starts at `70%`. Start with three first-order spells, one mastered and two unmastered, plus two second-order spells, one mastered and one unmastered. |

## Disadvantages

A disadvantage is a notable limitation that causes problems in play. Investigators using experience packages must choose one disadvantage. NPCs may also use disadvantages, but should usually have no more than one and must follow the same limits.

| Disadvantage | Limits | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blunt | None | Penalty die on all Social skill rolls. |
| Crossed the Wrong People | None | The GM chooses powerful adversaries who feature in play. If the adversary is removed, the GM should introduce an appropriate replacement. |
| Easily Distracted | None | Penalty die on Observation and Read Person rolls. |
| Damaged | May not be taken with Steadfast. | Rolls of `95+` are fumbles. When impaired by mental trauma or in combat, the fumble range becomes `90+`; recovery returns it to `95+`, not `100`. |
| Disorganised | None | Penalty die on Luck rolls to find useful carried items or useful items at the current location. |
| Fingers and Thumbs | None | Penalty die on DEX rolls involving manual dexterity. Penalty die on damage rolls with firearms and ranged weapons. Does not affect DEX rolls based on speed, such as initiative or Burst of Speed. |
| Frail | May not be taken with Natural Toughness. | The investigator has no Hurt damage level. `1` damage marks Bloodied. `2` damage marks Bloodied and Down. |
| Hot-Headed | None | Cannot choose the Flee combat option. Involuntary actions caused by fumbles or failed impairment POW rolls always involve violence. |
| Iron-Blooded | Cannot be taken by fae investigators. | Genii locorum, unicorns, and High Fae react badly to the investigator. They cannot form friendships with those beings, struggle to make deals with them, and take a penalty die on Social rolls with anyone vulnerable to cold iron. |
| Magic is Exhausting | None | The base cost of every spell the investigator knows increases by `1` magic point. Boost costs are unchanged. |
| Not a Magical Bone | May not be taken with Magical or by fae investigators. | The investigator cannot learn or use magic. They remain susceptible to spells and spell-like effects. |
| Slow-Footed | May not be taken with Speedy. | MOV starts at `5` before age adjustments. |
| Slow Healer | None | May not spend Luck to avoid the after-effects of being Down or while recovering from a Mortal Wound. May still spend Luck to turn a fatal blow into a mortal wound. |
| Weak | Maximum STR `40`. | Penalty die on all STR rolls, including melee damage rolls. |

## Creating New Occupations

If an existing occupation almost fits, adjust that occupation with the minimum changes needed.

If a new occupation is needed, define:

- Required skills, usually one to three.
- Recommended skills.
- Likely contacts.
- Starting equipment.
- How the occupation fits the investigator group and case premise.

Anything beyond those elements is mostly backstory rather than rules.

### Antiques Dealer

Use this as a sample custom occupation.

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Required skills | Appraise, Art/Craft (any). |
| Recommended skills | History, Languages, Observation, Research, Social. |
| Contacts | Booksellers, antique collectors, historians, historical societies, possibly criminal fences. |
| Equipment | Auction catalogues, jeweller's loupe, notebook, business cards. |

## Troupe Play

Troupe play lets each player control more than one investigator. It is useful for small player counts, split scenes, and cases that need a wider skill range than the primary investigators possess.

### Primary Investigators

Each player creates one primary investigator. Primary investigators are the main focus for that player and appear in most scenes.

### Secondary Investigators

Secondary investigators may be created by the players, by the GM, or by the group. Prefer one character per player in any given scene; if a player's primary and secondary investigators are both present, one should stay in the background.

| Type | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Alternate | A second investigator for occasional use. Create normally, but make them meaningfully different from the player's primary investigator. |
| Significant Other | A friend, relative, partner, or other important person in another primary investigator's life. Use only after checking group consent and boundaries. Any player may opt out or change their mind without needing to justify it. |
| Burner | A case-specific, high-risk secondary investigator intended to go into danger. The GM should define what kinds of burner characters fit the case. |
| Pool | A shared group of secondary investigators built from the primary investigators' close circle. Any player may portray a pool investigator when appropriate. |

### Character Knowledge

When one player controls more than one investigator, separate player knowledge from character knowledge.

Options:

- Keep each investigator's knowledge separate.
- Let the other investigator act only on a hunch or minor instinct, not on the full information.
- Establish a plausible off-screen exchange between investigators, such as a call or text, if time and circumstances allow.

## Investigator Organisations

An investigator organisation gives the group a reason to work together and a base of operations. It may be the Folly, a new branch, a non-police organisation, or an informal group.

When creating an organisation, define:

- Who its members are.
- Where it is based.
- What its base of operations is.
- What resources it can access.

### Structure

Most organisations have a leader. For play, an NPC leader often works better than a player-character leader because it avoids one player routinely directing the others.

Common structures:

| Structure | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Cell | A self-contained unit inside a larger organisation. Compromise of the cell does not expose the whole organisation. Resources may be limited or indirect. |
| Hierarchy | A top-down organisation. Resources may be stronger, but oversight and command pressure are more direct. |
| Informal | A loose group bound by shared experience, location, interest, or need. Resources depend on personal contacts and local access. |

### Local Folly Games

A campaign can move the Folly premise outside London. A new Folly branch in Great Britain is usually easier to justify than an overseas operation, where jurisdiction becomes a major issue.

Early cases for a new local branch can be small while the investigators learn the area. Later cases may reveal wider patterns or a larger antagonist.

### Non-Police Organisations

The investigators do not have to be police or law enforcement. A group could be students, family members, occult researchers, community figures, or another organisation that deals with supernatural threats.

### Base of Operations

A base may be formal, informal, fixed, or mobile. It may provide only basic shelter and meeting space, or it may include resources such as records, magical protections, a library, an armoury, specialist equipment, vehicles, or local contacts. Keep the base consistent with the organisation's funding, access, and story position.

## Skills Revisited

### Creating New Skills

Before creating a new expert skill, check whether an existing skill can be adjusted. If not, define:

- What the skill does.
- Whether another skill opposes it.
- What counts as Regular and Hard difficulty.
- How the roll can be pushed.
- Consequences for failing a pushed roll.

### Anthropology

Anthropology is a sample new expert skill.

Use:

- Identify and understand an individual or culture's way of life through observation and study.
- After a month or more observing a culture or studying accurate records, make simple predictions about behaviour, values, and likely responses.
- Combined with Read Person, may help predict the actions and beliefs of people from the studied culture.

Difficulty:

| Difficulty | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Regular | Predicting behaviour after a month or more of study. |
| Hard | Predicting behaviour after a week or less of study. |

Pushing examples:

- Spend more time studying.
- Immerse fully in the culture.
- Take part in a rite, ceremony, or social practice.

Failed pushed-roll consequences can include social transgression, hostility from the studied group, or harmful effects from a risky ceremony or substance.

### Combined Skill Rolls

Use one percentile roll when a situation involves multiple skills.

The GM states whether:

- All listed skills must succeed.
- Any one listed skill may succeed.

Compare the same dice result against each relevant skill. Do not make separate rolls for each skill; using a single roll preserves the intended probability.

### Social Skill Difficulty

For Social rolls, set difficulty from the opposing character's Read Person skill.

| Opposing Read Person | Social difficulty |
| --- | --- |
| Below `70%` | Regular |
| `70%+` | Hard |

Adjust as follows:

- If the NPC is already inclined toward the investigator's goal, no roll is needed.
- If the NPC is opposed to the goal, apply a penalty die.
- If the NPC is neutral but the request costs them effort, risk, or inconvenience, call for a roll.
- Strong leverage, a persuasive argument, or a meaningful bribe may grant a bonus die.
- At the edge of what a Social roll can achieve, the GM may require a Hard roll with a penalty die or rule that the NPC cannot be persuaded by that approach.

## Magic Revisited

### Higher-Order Spells

Higher-order spells are usually GM-facing effects, not normal investigator options. They may have no listed prerequisites if they are not intended for player use.

### Dissimulo

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Order | Eighth |
| Base cost | `8` magic points |
| Prerequisite | None listed; not intended for player use. |
| Duration | `10` minutes. |
| Resistance | Opposed POW roll against the target. |
| Boost | Each additional magic point increases duration by `10` minutes. |

Effect:

- If the caster loses the opposed POW roll, the spell fails.
- If the caster wins, the target's facial structure and flesh alter to the caster's design. The target suffers intense pain.
- When the spell ends, the altered structure collapses. The target immediately suffers Terminal damage.
- The spell can theoretically be self-cast, but doing so is a dire edge case.
- The spell ends automatically if the caster falls asleep.

### Creating New Newtonian Spells

When creating a spell, define:

- Order and base magic point cost.
- Prerequisites.
- Effect.
- Duration, if any.
- Resistance roll, if the spell affects another character's body, mind, autonomy, or behaviour.
- Range and base damage, if the spell is a ranged attack.
- Whether it can be boosted.
- What each boost does.
- Whether mastery changes the effect.

Guidance:

- If the source lists formae, use them to estimate order.
- Adjectivia and inflectentes do not count toward order.
- If order is unclear, compare the effect to existing spells or to when a similar spell appears in the source fiction.
- Prerequisites should follow from formae. For example, a second-order spell using `lux` should require Werelight or Flash Bulb.
- Test new spells through NPC use before making them available to investigators.

## Enchantments

Enchantments are GM-facing magical objects. The rules below provide handling guidance rather than a player crafting system.

### Wizard's Staff

A wizard's staff is a rechargeable magic point store.

Rules:

- There is no standard investigator crafting procedure for a staff.
- Creating one involves magical metalwork, a third-order spell used during forging, and well over `100` hours of work.
- A staff may be awarded, inherited, recovered, or built into a case at GM discretion.
- If the creator forged the staff themselves, its maximum magic point store is the creator's maximum magic points at the time of creation. It may store fewer.
- If the creator is unknown or someone else forged it, assume `10` magic points for a beginner's staff or `20` for an experienced practitioner's staff.
- Recharging requires two hours of quiet concentration.
- A staff fails to recharge only if its core is damaged or broken.
- When casting, the wielder chooses whether to spend their own magic points or the staff's stored points.
- If one source runs out while powering a spell, the spell automatically draws from the other until that source is also drained.

### Demon Trap

A demon trap is a magical anti-personnel mine. It is not an investigator crafting option.

Creation notes:

- Creating one requires only a second-order spell, but also requires torture and the trapping of a spirit.
- Treat it as a weaponised angry ghost.
- It may be made from metal or suitable plastics.
- It is identified more by magical feel than appearance.

Trigger:

- A demon trap uses two contacts.
- Stepping onto the trap primes it.
- Stepping off releases it.
- If an unaware character moves close to a demon trap, the GM calls for a Luck roll. Failure means they step on it and activate the first trigger.
- A practitioner who steps on a trap may sense it and get a chance to disarm it.

When a demon trap goes off, determine its effect and power level.

| Trap | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Standard physical blast | Terminal damage within `2` metres/yards; Deadly damage up to `6` metres/yards. |
| Greater physical blast | Splat damage within `2` metres/yards; Terminal damage up to `6` metres/yards. |
| Standard psychic wave | Everyone within `6` metres/yards makes a POW roll for Hyperthaumaturgical Degradation. Targets within `2` metres/yards take one penalty die. |
| Greater psychic wave | Everyone within `12` metres/yards makes a POW roll for Hyperthaumaturgical Degradation. Targets within `4` metres/yards take one penalty die. |

Locating a trap after stepping on it:

| Magic roll | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Success | The practitioner realises the first trigger is active. They cannot move without setting off the trap and may attempt to disarm it. |
| Failure | The practitioner does not realise they have triggered the trap; it goes off. |

Searching for a known trap:

- A practitioner who knows a trap is present but not exactly where may search using a fresh-cut branch.
- Roll Sense Vestigia or Magic, whichever is higher.

| Roll | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Success | The trap is located without activation. |
| Failure | The trap is located, but the first trigger is activated. The practitioner cannot move without setting it off and may attempt to disarm it. |

Disarming:

- A located demon trap must be disarmed with Sīphōnem.
- If neither trigger is active and the casting roll fails, the first trigger activates. The practitioner cannot move away without setting off the trap and gets one more chance to disarm it.
- If the first trigger is already active and the casting roll fails, the second trigger activates and the trap goes off.
- If the spell succeeds, the trap is deactivated and its stored energy is drained.
- Disarming or triggering a trap may be sensed by practitioners and demi-monde beings within `800` metres / `0.5` miles. A very powerful trap may be felt farther away.
- Demon traps commonly include a secondary charge to catch rescuers.

If the first trigger is active and no one present knows Sīphōnem, call someone who does, evacuate the area, or try to reduce the harm when the trap goes off.

| Escape attempt | Hard DEX success |
| --- | --- |
| Standard physical trap | Reduce damage from Terminal to Deadly. |
| Standard psychic trap | Remove the penalty die from the HTD POW roll. |
| Greater physical trap | Reduce damage from Splat to Terminal. |
| Greater psychic trap | No benefit; the effect range is too large. |

### Rose Jars

Rose jars trap ghosts or similar entities.

Rules:

- An occupied jar emits a soft glow.
- If the jar is smashed, the entity inside is destroyed.
- If the jar is opened more carefully, it cracks and loses one-quarter to one-half of its liquid. The ghost appears, behaves like a ghost, then breaks down and vanishes.
- If the resident ghost permits it, touching the jar can draw a viewer's consciousness into the ghost's miniature internal realm.
- A viewer inside that realm may speak with the ghost and ask questions for as long as the ghost permits.
- A ghost is never required to admit a viewer.

Use Rose jars as unusual witnesses, dangerous evidence, or items that must be recovered before collectors or unlicensed practitioners misuse them.

### Non-Newtonian NPC Abilities

When creating magic from a non-Newtonian tradition, do not build a full alternate magic system by default.

Options:

- Reskin an existing Chapter 4 spell while keeping its magic point cost, Magic roll, range, damage, and other mechanics.
- Create a narrow special ability with a fixed magic point cost.
- Tie the tradition to a modified advantage, enchanted item, discipline, or demi-monde-style affinity.

Keep each tradition recognisable and limited. Avoid making one NPC tradition good at every kind of magic.

## Combat Revisited

All rules in this section are optional.

### Surprise

If combatants are openly facing off, start the combat round when the first action is declared. Use surprise only when an attack is genuinely unexpected or a new combatant enters by stealth.

A character who has already been seen in or participated in the combat cannot make another surprise attack against opponents who know they are present.

Drugged, unconscious, or otherwise helpless targets are defenceless; the GM adjudicates the result.

#### Attacking With Surprise

| Situation | Roll | Success | Failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attacker is sneaking up on the target. | Attacker's Stealth, with difficulty set by target's Observation. If target Observation is `70%+`, difficulty is Hard. | Attack has surprise and automatically hits; roll damage. | Resolve attack normally. |
| Target knows the attacker is present, but not that the attack is coming. | Attacker's Stealth, with difficulty set by target's higher of Observation or Read Person. If that skill is `70%+`, difficulty is Hard. | Attack has surprise and automatically hits; roll damage. | Resolve attack normally. |

#### Defending Against Surprise

| Situation | Roll | Success | Failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attacker is sneaking up on the investigator. | Investigator's Observation, with difficulty set by attacker's Stealth. If attacker Stealth is `70%+`, difficulty is Hard. | Anticipate the attack; resolve normally. | The attacker has surprise and automatically hits; roll damage. |
| Investigator knows the attacker is present, but not that the attack is coming. | Investigator's higher of Observation or Read Person, with difficulty set by attacker's Stealth. If attacker Stealth is `70%+`, difficulty is Hard. | Anticipate the attack; resolve normally. | The attacker has surprise and automatically hits; roll damage. |

### Prone Revisited

Additional prone rules:

- A prone character normally stands on their next turn as a free action.
- A character holding an action may interrupt to keep a prone character down. If the interrupt succeeds, the prone character must win an opposed Fighting roll to stand. This uses both characters' turns for the round.
- A prone character gains one bonus die on Firearms rolls.
- Attacks against a prone target with a gun or spell take one penalty die, unless the attack is at point-blank range.

### Holding an Action

A character may hold their turn in the round.

Options:

- Act after a named character's turn in the same round.
- Wait to interrupt another character's future action.

If the held action is not used before the character's normal turn in the next round, the held action is lost and the character may act normally on the new turn.

### Interrupting

A held action may interrupt another character's action, such as breaking cover, attacking an ally, or trying to stand while prone.

Rules:

- Roll opposed DEX to determine who acts first.
- On a tie, the character holding the action acts first.
- Use the same opposed DEX rule for melee, firearms, magic, movement, or other actions.
- A held character can target someone who briefly breaks cover.
- A held character can intervene when an enemy attacks an ally.

If a player wants to shield an ally from a spell or bullet without having held an action, the GM may allow them to use their Fight Back response on the ally's behalf. A character can still make Fight Back only once per round unless they have Fast Reactions.

### Automatic Fire

Full auto represents emptying a clip on one turn.

Rules:

- Full auto is separate from Multiple Shots.
- The firearm must have more than half its ammunition remaining.
- Full auto usually empties the clip.
- Weapons with belts, drums, or similar larger ammunition capacity may make up to `3` auto-fire attacks across multiple rounds before reloading.
- If ammunition remaining is uncertain, the GM may call for a Luck roll to see whether the firearm has enough ammunition for full auto.

#### Spray and Pray

Spray and pray covers an arc of fire up to `90` degrees.

Rules:

- The GM decides which targets are inside the arc.
- Targets beyond `50` metres/yards are ignored.
- Each target may dive for cover. A target that dives automatically avoids damage, but sacrifices their next turn. If they already acted this round, they sacrifice their turn in the following round.
- If every target dives for cover, no attack roll is needed.
- If any target does not dive, roll Firearms with one penalty die. Set difficulty by range as normal.
- If most targets are at point-blank range, the GM may grant one bonus die, cancelling the full-auto penalty die.

| Firearms result | Targets hit |
| --- | ---: |
| Critical success | `6` |
| Hard success | `3` |
| Regular success | `1` |
| Failure | `0` |

Assigning hits:

- Go around the table and nominate targets, GM first.
- A target can be hit only once by the same spray-and-pray attack.
- If hits remain after all eligible targets have been assigned, the remaining shots miss.
- Each NPC hit takes `1` damage plus the firearm's base weapon damage.
- Each investigator hit makes a Luck roll. Success means only `1` damage; failure means `1` damage plus the firearm's base weapon damage.

#### Concentrated Fire

Concentrated fire focuses full auto on one target or on a tight group.

Targets:

- Single target: one individual.
- Group target: a central target plus one target on each side.
- The two side targets must be within arm's reach of the central target.
- The whole group must be within a `2` metre/yard-wide zone.

Rules:

- Set Firearms difficulty as normal.
- Apply normal modifiers such as point-blank range or target speed.
- Do not use Multiple Shots.
- Concentrated fire grants one bonus die, cancelling the full-auto penalty die.
- If the attack succeeds, roll damage separately for each target.
- If firing at a single target, double the damage roll before adding base weapon damage.
- If firing at a group, roll each target's damage normally.
- In a packed narrow space, the GM may include more targets within the `2` metre/yard zone.
- A shooter using full auto cannot pick out one individual from a close-packed group.

### Firearms Extras

#### Sights and Bracing

Using telescopic sights together with effective bracing doubles the firearm's standard range and maximum effective range.

Examples of bracing include a mounted weapon, bipod, tripod, sandbags, or another effective support.

Sights provide no benefit at point-blank range.

#### Complete Concealment

If a target remains fully concealed for the whole round, an attacker may still fire through the cover or into the concealed area.

Depending on the size of the area and available clues to the target's position, the GM may:

- Raise the difficulty.
- Apply a penalty die.
- Raise the difficulty and apply a penalty die.
- Allow a hit only on a Critical success, or not at all.

If the attack hits, reduce damage using the protection provided by the cover material.

### Armour Revisited

For ordinary rough-and-tumble harm, armour on a specific body part may simply reduce damage if that makes narrative sense.

For a single precise attack where the question is whether armour covered the hit:

- Make a Luck roll.
- Success means the armour was hit and reduces damage.
- Failure means the attack struck an unarmoured area.
- Armour protecting both head and torso grants one bonus die on this Luck roll.
- All-over body armour or natural armour applies without a Luck roll.
- A known vulnerable point in armour can be targeted, but the GM should raise the difficulty or apply a penalty die.

### Damage Rolls: Alternative Method

This optional method replaces the damage roll with relative success levels.

For opposed attacks:

| Result | Damage |
| --- | --- |
| Same success level, attacker wins because it is their turn | `1` damage; do not add base weapon damage. |
| Attacker wins by 1 success level | `1` damage plus base weapon damage. |
| Attacker wins by 2 success levels | `2` damage plus base weapon damage. |
| Attacker wins by 3 success levels | `3` damage plus base weapon damage. |
| Attacker wins by 4 success levels | `4` damage plus base weapon damage. |

For unopposed attacks:

| Result | Damage |
| --- | --- |
| Attacker achieves exactly the required success level | `1` damage; do not add base weapon damage. |
| Attacker achieves 1 success level above the requirement | `1` damage plus base weapon damage. |
| Attacker achieves 2 success levels above the requirement | `2` damage plus base weapon damage. |

Examples of opposed margins:

| Margin | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| 1 level | Weak vs fumble; Regular vs Weak; Hard vs Regular; Critical vs Hard. |
| 2 levels | Regular vs fumble; Hard vs Weak; Critical vs Regular. |
| 3 levels | Hard vs fumble; Critical vs Weak. |
| 4 levels | Critical vs fumble. |

As normal, `4` damage in one blow is a mortal wound, and `5` damage in one attack is a fatal blow.

## Wounds and Poisons Revisited

### Spending Luck to Resist Incapacitation

A character who suffers `3+` damage in combat is normally Down. With this optional rule, they may spend Luck to remain active briefly.

| Cost | Effect |
| ---: | --- |
| `5` Luck | Remain active until the end of the current round. |
| `5` additional Luck | Remain active for one more round; pay at the start of that round. |

Limits:

- If the character takes further damage, they are immediately Down and can no longer resist incapacitation.
- A character with a Mortal Wound may use the same Luck spend, but the GM decides what actions are still physically possible.

### Detailed Poisons

Use this optional system when a specific poison matters.

All poisons have:

- A type: very mild, mild, strong, or lethal.
- A speed.
- A one-dose effect.

Rules:

- Multiple doses add one penalty die to the victim's CON roll to resist.
- The GM decides whether symptoms prevent action, allow action with a penalty die, or increase difficulty.
- When poisoned, the victim makes a CON roll.

| CON result | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Failure | Apply the poison's listed severity. |
| Regular success | Reduce severity by one level. |
| Hard success | Reduce severity by two levels. |
| Critical success | GM may allow the poison to be shaken off entirely. |

If the CON roll fails:

| Poison type | Damage effect |
| --- | --- |
| Very mild | No damage; temporary unconsciousness for `1D6` minutes or rounds, as appropriate. |
| Mild | `2` damage; recovery takes about 24 hours. |
| Strong | `4` damage, causing a Mortal Wound. |
| Lethal | Make another CON roll. Failure means immediate death; success means a Mortal Wound. |

Fast poisons may take effect within a single round. Slower poisons may take hours or days.

### Sample Poisons

| Poison | Type | Speed | Effect, one dose | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amanita (agaric mushrooms) | Mild | 6 to 24 hours | Violent stomach pains, vomiting, jaundice; suffer `2` damage and recover in about 24 hours. | Death cap mushrooms are the main fatal example. |
| Arsenic | Lethal | 30 minutes to 24 hours | Burning pain, vomiting, violent diarrhoea; make another CON roll. Failure means death; success means a mortal wound. | Historically hard to identify before the Marsh Test. |
| Belladonna (deadly nightshade) | Lethal | 2 hours to 2 days | Rapid heartbeat, impaired vision, convulsions; make another CON roll. Failure means death; success means a mortal wound. | `10-20` berries can probably kill an adult. |
| Black widow spider venom (latrotoxin) | Strong | 2 to 8 hours | Chills, sweats, nausea; suffer `4` damage, causing a mortal wound. | Rarely fatal with medical treatment. |
| Chloroform | Very mild | 1 round | Unconsciousness for 1 hour; no damage. | A high-strength toxic dose may be fatal. |
