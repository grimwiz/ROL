# Core Resolution

<!-- Source: core-rules, skills, rules-summaries. Status: extracted-draft. -->

## When to Roll

The GM calls for a roll when uncertainty, opposition, risk, or dramatic pressure makes the outcome matter.

Do not roll for routine actions, obvious clues, or tasks that a competent character can complete without meaningful pressure. A failed roll should change the situation, add cost, create danger, or move the case in a new direction.

Before rolling:

- State the acting character's goal.
- Confirm the relevant skill or characteristic.
- Confirm whether the roll is unopposed, opposed, or a Luck roll.
- Set Regular or Hard difficulty if unopposed.

The GM has final authority over when a roll is needed and which skill, characteristic, difficulty, or opposition applies.

## Choosing a Roll

Use the most specific relevant skill. If no listed skill applies:

- Use an expert skill if the task clearly requires specialist training.
- Use a characteristic if general ability is the best fit.
- Use INT for general knowledge, memory, deduction, puzzles, or identifying present-day languages when no specific skill is more appropriate.
- Use Luck only when chance or external circumstance is the main question.

## Difficulty

Unopposed rolls use two difficulty levels.

| Difficulty | Target | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Regular | Full skill or characteristic | Default for a competent challenge. |
| Hard | Half skill or characteristic | Professional-grade or exceptional challenge. |

Easy tasks normally require no roll.

For a living opponent, the GM can either use an opposed roll or set difficulty from the opposing skill/characteristic:

| Opposing value | Difficulty |
|---:|---|
| Below 70 | Regular |
| 70 or higher | Hard |

For non-living obstacles, use Regular unless the obstacle is exceptional.

## Success Levels

| Level | Roll result |
|---|---|
| Critical success | `01` |
| Hard success | Equal to or below half value |
| Regular success | Equal to or below full value |
| Failure | Above full value |
| Fumble | `100`, or `90+` while impaired |

A Regular success does not satisfy a Hard difficulty roll. Critical and Hard successes may create extra benefit if the situation supports it.

In opposed rolls, failure is called Weak success because it can still beat a fumble.

## Fumbles

A fumble is worse than an ordinary failure. The GM chooses an appropriate complication, such as:

- Involuntary action.
- Equipment failure or loss.
- Vehicle trouble on a Drive roll.
- Firearm malfunction on a Firearms roll.
- Discovery, exposure, injury, or a new threat.

Luck cannot alter a fumble.

## Involuntary Actions

On a fumble or failed impairment POW roll, the GM may choose an involuntary action or roll `1D10`.

| D10 | Action |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Drop held item. |
| 2 | Cry out in fear or frustration. |
| 3 | Say something incriminating, inappropriate, or unwise. |
| 4 | Freeze. |
| 5 | Sob or laugh uncontrollably. |
| 6 | Lash out, throw a held weapon, or pull a trigger. |
| 7 | Back away or hide. |
| 8 | Endanger a friend through words or action. |
| 9 | Confess, beg, or ask forgiveness. |
| 10 | Become enraged and break something. |

## Pushing Rolls

After a failed skill or characteristic roll, the player may either spend Luck or push the roll if the situation allows it.

A pushed roll is a second and final attempt at the same goal.

Requirements:

- The player describes a changed approach, extra effort, extra time, or increased risk.
- The goal must still be achievable.
- The skill and difficulty normally stay the same unless the fiction has changed.
- Time passes between the original roll and the pushed roll.

Limits:

- Luck rolls cannot be pushed.
- Opposed rolls cannot be pushed.
- Combat rolls cannot be pushed.
- Pushed roll results cannot be altered with Luck.

Failure on a pushed roll gives the GM licence to impose a harder consequence. Possible consequences include damage, loss of equipment, exposure, delay, separation, capture, escalation, or achieving the goal at a serious cost.

The GM may foreshadow likely consequences before the pushed roll, especially if the character would understand the risk.

## Multiple Characters and One Task

The GM decides how many characters can contribute.

Use these patterns:

| Situation | Handling |
|---|---|
| Any one success solves it | Everyone positioned to help may roll; one success is enough. |
| Restricted access or single tool/source | One character rolls; another attempt usually costs time or requires pushing. |
| Group physical effort | Everyone involved rolls; the highest relevant value must succeed and no helper may fumble. |
| Group stealth or similar weak-link task | The lowest relevant skill rolls. |
| Group Luck | The present character with the lowest Luck rolls. |

Repeated attempts at the same goal by the same character usually require pushing.

## Bonus and Penalty Dice

Use bonus or penalty dice for circumstances around the task. Use difficulty for the inherent challenge of the task.

- A bonus die means rolling an extra tens die and keeping the lower/better tens result.
- A penalty die means rolling an extra tens die and keeping the higher/worse tens result.
- One bonus die cancels one penalty die.
- Bonus and penalty dice do not stack beyond one net bonus or one net penalty on a skill or characteristic roll.
- Opposed rolls do not use difficulty levels; apply a bonus or penalty die to the relevant side if circumstances give a clear edge or drawback.

Difficulty and bonus/penalty dice can both be adjusted, but that should be exceptional.

## Human Limits

When an unopposed roll is set by an opposing skill or characteristic:

- Opposition below 70 gives Regular difficulty.
- Opposition 70 or higher gives Hard difficulty.
- A Hard success can overcome opposition up to `100 + acting value`.
- Opposition above `100 + acting value` is impossible for that character; no roll is allowed.

Assistance may bring an impossible or Hard task within reach. The GM decides how many helpers can reasonably contribute.

## Luck Rolls

Use Luck for external chance, coincidence, preparation uncertainty, or availability when no skill or characteristic applies.

To make a Luck roll, roll equal to or below current Luck.

Group Luck:

- If a Luck roll affects the group, the present investigator with the lowest current Luck rolls.
- If a random bad event must hit one investigator, the GM may assign it to the present investigator with the lowest current Luck.

Luck rolls cannot be altered by spending Luck.

## Spending Luck

An investigator's current Luck is also a spendable point pool.

After a skill or characteristic roll, Luck may be spent one-for-one to reduce the dice result.

Allowed uses:

- Improve an investigator's own skill or characteristic roll.
- Improve a Magic roll.
- Improve opposed or unopposed combat rolls.
- Buy a Critical success by reducing the roll to `01`.
- Improve a damage roll by improving the damage roll's success level.
- Prevent death from a fatal blow by spending 30 Luck.
- Reduce wound effects after a fight under the healing rules.

Limits:

- Spend no more Luck than the investigator currently has.
- Spent Luck immediately reduces current Luck.
- Luck cannot alter Luck rolls.
- Luck cannot alter fumbles.
- Luck cannot alter pushed rolls.
- Luck cannot alter another character's roll.
- Opposed and combat rolls cannot be pushed, so Luck is the only normal post-roll correction option.

Spending Luck on a damage roll improves the roll's success level; it does not add damage point-for-point.

## Trying Your Luck

If a required expert skill is absent among the investigators present, an investigator may spend 10 Luck to attempt the task.

Procedure:

1. Spend 10 Luck.
2. Use half of the linked characteristic as the target for this one expert-skill roll.
3. If the roll fails, the investigator may spend more Luck to improve it or may push the roll.

If the roll succeeds and no one else in the group has that expert skill, the investigator may spend another 10 Luck to add the expert skill permanently at half the linked characteristic.

Limits:

- Total minimum cost to both attempt and keep the skill is 20 Luck.
- More Luck may be spent if needed to make the roll succeed.
- Each investigator can acquire at most one new expert skill per game session this way.
- Magic cannot be acquired this way; the Magical advantage is required first.

## Recovering Luck

After every session, each investigator makes a Luck improvement check:

1. Roll `1D100`.
2. If the roll is above current Luck, add `1D10` Luck.
3. If the roll is equal to or below current Luck, Luck is unchanged.

Once per case file, an investigator may take a substantial rest and relaxation break to gain `1D10` Luck automatically.

At the start of each new case file:

1. Roll `2D10 + 50`.
2. Compare the result to current Luck.
3. If the result is higher, current Luck becomes that result.
4. If the result is equal to or lower, current Luck is unchanged.

Starting Luck is not a cap and can be exceeded in play.

## Opposed Rolls

Use opposed rolls when two characters directly contest an outcome.

Procedure:

1. Each side states a goal.
2. The GM approves the skill or characteristic for each side; they need not be the same.
3. Both sides roll.
4. Higher success level wins.

Success level order:

```text
Critical > Hard > Regular > Weak success > Fumble
```

Opposed rolls should produce a winner and loser. A failed roll counts as Weak success if the other side fumbles.

Opposed rolls cannot be pushed.

## Opposed Roll Ties

If both sides have the same success level:

1. A player may spend Luck to improve their success level above the opponent's result.
2. If still tied, the higher relevant skill value wins.
3. In combat, the character whose turn it is wins the tie instead of comparing skill values.
4. If still tied outside combat, the GM may let the tie stand or use a random tiebreaker.

## Impairment and Rolls

While impaired:

- Any skill or characteristic roll of 90 or higher is a fumble.
- Luck cannot alter that fumble.
- If the GM judges the impaired character is under fresh stress, a Regular POW roll may be required to maintain composure.
- A roll of 90 or higher on that composure check causes an involuntary action.

Mental trauma can cause impairment. The GM calls for a Regular POW roll when an investigator experiences stress, shock, fear, horror, gunfire, a serious injury to an ally, death, a grisly corpse, or a shocking supernatural manifestation. Failure causes impairment and an involuntary action.

An investigator is either impaired or not; additional impairment has no extra effect until recovery.

Recovery from impairment is covered in `07-damage-and-healing.md`.

## Failure Without Dead Ends

Failed rolls should keep play moving where possible. Prefer complications, cost, capture, loss, delay, bargains, injury, changed circumstances, or partial success over outcomes that end the investigation.
