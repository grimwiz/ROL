# Keeper Procedures

Guidance for preparing and running *Call of Cthulhu*. Player-facing rules are in the numbered `rules/` files.

## Tone and Goal

Call of Cthulhu is a game of **mood and horror**, not combat. The investigators' purpose is to uncover and oppose the Mythos; the long-term goal of a campaign may be to forestall the Great Old Ones. Keep play full of bumps in the night, sinister strangers, and dread. Draw ideas from horror fiction and film, especially Lovecraft and writers in his circle (and his essay *Supernatural Horror in Literature*).

## Running Monsters

- Describe, don't name: present "a froglike half-human thing draped in seaweed, stinking of dead fish," not "a Deep One." Let the players' Cthulhu Mythos skill do the identifying.
- Many Mythos beings cannot realistically be beaten — the goal is often to **avoid and escape**. Foreshadow horror (a blast of cold air, distant howling, the whip-poor-wills falling silent) so encounters are not random, but not every cue should mean a monster.
- Make guns ineffective against true Mythos threats: characters who stand and "blaze away" instead of fleeing should be the ones who die. Limit gunfire to special situations.
- **Escalate slowly:** start with fanatic cults and lesser races, work up to greater races, and only introduce a deity when the investigators are ready.

## Pacing and Lethality

- The game is already deadly through insanity; do not kill central investigators gratuitously. When a character faints, let him lie rather than be devoured; when something must take a victim, prefer an NPC hireling.
- Enforce the SAN rule rigidly: at **SAN 0** a character becomes a Keeper-run NPC. Insane former investigators make good antagonists in later scenarios.
- Mix in the occasional hoax, dead-end trail, or "normal" threat (a mundane ghost or werewolf, Prohibition gangsters, spies) so the world is not wall-to-wall Mythos.

## Structure and Motive

- Give each investigator a reason to take the case — a family secret, a professional assignment (a journalist's editor simply sends them), a personal stake.
- Craft events deliberately; **avoid random-encounter and wandering-monster tables**. Remain flexible and adapt the prepared scenario to the players' plans.

## Group Size

Call of Cthulhu does not suit solitaire play but works very well with a Keeper and one or two players; keep groups small — three or four players is plenty.

## Money for a Campaign

Investigators are funded by their income and savings (see `10-equipment-and-the-1920s.md`), supplemented by patrons, institutions, or rewards. Credit Rating governs loans and financial standing.
