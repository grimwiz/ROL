# Skills

<!-- Advanced corpus: derived from rules/04-skills.md. -->

This file is the canonical skill reference. Character creation values are summarised here and fully routed through `03-character-creation.md`; rolling procedure, difficulty, pushing, opposed rolls, and Luck are in `02-core-resolution.md`.

## Skill Categories

| Category | Base value | Creation rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Common | 30 | All investigators have these. A selected starting skill becomes 60. |
| Combat | 30 | Fighting and Firearms. A selected starting skill becomes 60. Combat skills cannot be pushed. |
| Expert | 00 | Only usable if selected, granted, or acquired. A selected starting skill becomes 60. |

Skill values also describe broad competence:

| Value | Competence |
|---:|---|
| Below 50 | Amateur ability or rudimentary training. |
| 50-69 | Professional competence. |
| 70-89 | Expert competence. |
| 90+ | Master-level competence. |

## Specialisations

Art/Craft, Languages, and Science are specialisation skills. A generic rating in the parent skill is not selected.

When Art/Craft, Languages, or Science is selected as one of the six boosted starting skills:

- Choose one specialisation at 60.
- Choose two other specialisations at 30 each.
- The skill may be selected again to add more specialisations, if the GM allows it.

If a related specialisation could plausibly apply, the GM may allow the roll with either increased difficulty or a penalty die. There must be meaningful overlap.

## Skill Difficulty Notes

Skill entries list common Regular and Hard uses. The normal rules still apply:

- Regular difficulty uses the full value.
- Hard difficulty uses the half value.
- If living opposition sets difficulty, opposition below 70 is Regular and opposition 70+ is Hard.
- The GM may use an opposed roll instead of fixed difficulty.
- Skill-specific pushing notes are examples, not new pushing procedures.

## Common Skills

### Athletics

Athletics covers climbing, jumping, swimming, throwing, gymnastics, field events, and similar physical activity.

Special uses:

- Simple strength feats may use STR or Athletics, whichever is higher.
- Simple sprints may use DEX or Athletics, whichever is higher.
- One successful Athletics roll usually completes a climb; longer or harder climbs increase difficulty rather than requiring repeated rolls.
- Falling: a successful Athletics roll used to break a fall halves falling damage, rounded down.
- Throwing: a palm-sized, reasonably balanced object can be thrown up to STR/5 metres/yards. Range may extend up to STR/2 metres/yards with a penalty die. Objects over 2 kg / 5 lbs halve these ranges.
- Throwing failure places the object away from the target at a distance the GM judges from the failed roll.
- Athletics is used for thrown attacks with suitable objects and weapons.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Climb a drainpipe, swim in a fast current, throw a ball through a hoop, leap down the character's own height, standing jump the character's own height, running jump twice the character's own height | Regular |
| Climb in rain, swim against rapids while clothed, throw from centre court, achieve roughly double the listed jumping distances | Hard |

Pushing: reassess the task, take a longer route, apply dangerous extra effort, or accept injury risk.

Limits on pushing:

- A thrown object cannot usually be pushed once released. If multiple missiles are available and failure still carries risk, the GM may allow a pushed roll.
- A failed jump over an abyss cannot be pushed after the fall has begun. Luck may still be spent if otherwise allowed.
- Repeated attempts to escape a pit by jumping or climbing can be summarised as one pushed roll.
- Swimming rolls are only needed in crisis or danger. A failed pushed swimming roll can cause drowning, being swept away, or unconsciousness.

### Drive

Drive covers cars and light trucks, ordinary manoeuvres, pursuit driving, tailing a vehicle, and ordinary vehicle problems.

Unusual vehicles or machinery, such as tractors, lorries, cranes, forklifts, and construction machinery, use Drive with a penalty die unless the vehicle fits the investigator's occupation or background.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Weave through light traffic at speed to avoid pursuit | Regular |
| Weave through heavy traffic at speed to avoid pursuit | Hard |

Pushing: drive at the vehicle's limit or refuse to slow despite risk. Failed pushed rolls can cause crashes, skids, immobilisation, police attention, or other vehicle trouble.

### Navigate

Navigate covers finding a route, staying oriented, using maps, charts, instruments, satellite location tools, cartography, and reading topographical maps.

The Knowledge advantage gives a bonus die to Navigate rolls in the selected city or area.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Keep to the right route or find a destination in the investigator's own city | Regular |
| Maintain direction with no clear route or landmarks; find the way through a totally unfamiliar city | Hard |

Pushing: stop to study a map, return to the start, take more time, or ask for directions. Failed pushed rolls can cause lost time, wrong turns, isolation, or entering danger.

### Observation

Observation covers mundane senses: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. It is used to notice hidden details, intruders, ambushes, altered scenes, hidden compartments, unusual smells, tampered food or drink, or overheard conversation.

Special uses:

- Fleeting chances, distraction, clutter, poor visibility, or a tiny target can increase difficulty.
- A thorough search may grant automatic success where time and access are sufficient.
- Observation can be rolled with a penalty die to read lips if the investigator lacks Read Lips.
- Observation opposes Stealth and Disguise.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Search a cluttered room, hear nearby approach with Stealth below 70, eavesdrop on a nearby conversation | Regular |
| Find something deliberately well hidden, hear approach with Stealth 70+, eavesdrop on whispers | Hard |

Pushing: spend more time, search destructively, silence or alter the environment, or listen again. Failed pushed rolls can leave signs of the search, miss a threat, create surprise, produce false information, or expose eavesdropping.

### Read Person

Read Person studies a person to infer motive, intent, deception, bluffing, emotional state, or whether a threat is real.

It can oppose Social, especially deception, and can help penetrate Disguise.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Read intent or lying against Social below 70 | Regular |
| Read intent or lying against Social 70+ | Hard |

Pushing: ask intrusive questions, study the target intensely, or gather and analyse personal data. Failed pushed rolls can reveal the investigator's motives, offend the target, expose the agenda to enemies, or impose exhaustion from obsessive study.

### Research

Research locates information through the internet, libraries, archives, databases, or other non-verbal sources. The GM sets the time required.

Special uses:

- If only a Regular success is required, a Hard success finds the information in half the normal time.
- Locked records, special collections, or restricted data may also require credentials or Social.
- Information from people uses Social, not Research.
- Obscure internet research may require combined Computer Use and Research.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Find a difficult book or piece of information in a library or online | Regular |
| Find information in a disorganised source or under severe time pressure | Hard |

Pushing: take more time, ask a librarian or IT contact, or work systematically through more data. Failed pushed rolls can waste time, produce misleading information, alert opposition, or cause fatigue.

### Sense Vestigia

Sense Vestigia detects magical residue and magical signatures.

Special uses:

- Human investigators without Magic have Sense Vestigia at 30.
- Human investigators who select Magic as a starting skill raise Sense Vestigia to 60 for free.
- Fae investigators begin with Sense Vestigia 60.
- Most encountered vestigia is strong and requires Regular success to detect all elements.
- Weak vestigia reveals its main element on a Regular success and all elements on a Hard success.
- Extremely strong vestigia usually requires no roll to notice.
- For strong vestigia, the GM may reveal one dominant element even on failure.
- Calling for this roll can itself signal that something strange is present.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Detect strong vestigia and all present elements | Regular |
| Detect weak vestigia and all present elements | Hard |

Pushing: concentrate longer, remove distractions, clear the area, or focus on the sensation. Failed pushed rolls can miss nearby danger or misidentify the vestigia.

### Social

Social covers persuasion, intimidation, seduction, flattery, bargaining, verbal trickery, and deception. The player states the approach and goal.

Limits and modifiers:

- Social cannot normally compel behaviour completely contrary to the target's nature.
- A bribe, gift, promise, weapon, threat, or leverage may reduce difficulty or grant a bonus die.
- Silver-Tongued grants a bonus die when using eloquent persuasion.
- Scary grants a bonus die when intimidating.
- Social used for deception is opposed by Read Person.
- Social used for intimidation or coercion may be opposed by POW.
- The GM may set difficulty from opposition or call for an opposed roll.

Contacts:

- Occupation contacts do not always require Social.
- If a roll is needed with an occupation contact, roll Social with a bonus die.
- If the request harms or burdens the contact, the GM may mark the relationship Sour and make the contact unavailable.
- Connected favours normally require no Social roll with the connection.
- If a Connected relationship turns Sour, repairing it may require a Hard Social roll; failure can leave the connection strained and useless.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Persuade a target with Social/POW below 70; secure help that inconveniences or costs the target | Regular |
| Persuade a target with Social/POW 70+; secure help that risks job, safety, reputation, or serious stakes | Hard |

Pushing: flatter openly, share a secret, increase leverage, or intensify a threat. Failed pushed rolls can end the conversation, alert enemies, sour the relationship, cause accidental harm, reveal secrets, or let the target turn the pressure back.

### Stealth

Stealth covers moving quietly, hiding, camouflage, covert techniques, patience, and remaining still under pressure.

Stealth is usually opposed by Observation. Darkness, noise, concealment, time pressure, surface, and other circumstances can modify the roll or call for an opposed roll.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Move quietly around average people with Observation below 70 | Regular |
| Sneak past an attentive guard dog, find cover in seconds, or bypass Observation 70+ | Hard |

Pushing: study the area longer, remove noisy clothing, change clothing, create a distraction, or wait for a better moment. Failed pushed rolls can sound an alarm, reveal the investigator, set up a surprise attack, or let the target secretly notice and respond.

## Expert Skills

Expert skills usually cannot be attempted without the skill. If no present investigator has the needed expert skill, the group can seek NPC help, use another information source, or use Trying Your Luck from `02-core-resolution.md`.

### Accounting

Accounting reveals financial condition, accountancy procedures, ledgers, spreadsheets, hidden payments, blackmail, fraud, bribes, siphoned funds, business health, and how money moved in the past.

If someone used Accounting to conceal the issue, use the concealer's Accounting to set difficulty.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Analyse complete, well-documented accounts | Regular |
| Analyse disorganised, partial, or deliberately obscured accounts; beat Accounting 70+ concealment | Hard |

Pushing: review documents longer, visit banks or businesses, interview relevant people, or recalculate. Failed pushed rolls can alert opposition, lose records, destroy evidence, or corrupt files.

### Animal Handling

Animal Handling commands and trains animals to perform simple tasks. It is most often used with domesticated animals, but the GM may allow other animals. Ride is used for riding animals and controlling mounts.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Command a trained dog to sit, fetch, or attack | Regular |
| Get an untrained or unfamiliar dog to follow a command | Hard |

Pushing: move closer or handle the animal directly. Failed pushed rolls can provoke an attack, loss of control, or escape.

### Appraise

Appraise estimates value, materials, workmanship, quality, age, historical relevance, and authenticity.

If detecting a forgery, the creator's Art/Craft (Forgery) sets difficulty.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Assess a common item of common material | Regular |
| Assess an unusual, rare, foreign, ancient, or uncommon-material item; detect Art/Craft (Forgery) 70+ | Hard |

Pushing: consult another expert, test the item, or research provenance. Failed pushed rolls can damage the item, attract thieves or rivals, or trigger the item's function.

### Art/Craft

Art/Craft is always taken as a specialisation. It covers knowledge of a field, its history, practitioners, techniques, performance, and creation or repair of works.

Creation rule:

- Choose one Art/Craft specialisation at 60.
- Choose two other Art/Craft specialisations at 30 each.

Common specialisations include:

- Acting
- Barbering
- Calligraphy
- Carpentry
- Cooking
- Dancing
- Embroidery
- Fine Art
- Forgery
- Morris Dancing
- Opera Singing
- Painting and Decorating
- Photography
- Pottery
- Sculpting
- Writing

Special uses:

- A successful roll can identify where, when, how, or by whom a work was made.
- A roll can create, repair, perform, or compose if time and tools are available.
- Higher success levels may indicate better quality or precision.
- Forgery is the specialisation for faking documents, signatures, permits, and similar items.
- Detecting a forgery usually uses Appraise opposed by Art/Craft (Forgery); ancient fakes may use Science (Archaeology).
- Acting supports impersonation and may combine with Disguise.
- Photography covers film, digital photography, video, development, enhancement, digital editing, manipulation detection, and determining camera angle or position. Ordinary snapshots require no roll.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Make a standard saleable work or performance | Regular |
| Make a high-quality, intricate, unusual, or technically demanding work | Hard |

Pushing: start over, research further, rework the piece, or consult another expert. Failed pushed rolls can waste time and money, offend an audience or patron, injure someone through the work, or damage reputation.

### Computer Use

Computer Use covers programming, obscure data retrieval, data analysis, secured-system intrusion, network exploration, back doors, viruses, and intrusion detection. Everyday computer use requires no roll.

Obscure internet research may require combined Computer Use and Research.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Write, analyse, or debug a program | Regular |
| Hack a secure network without identification or tracing | Hard |

Pushing: take longer, reuse code, or deploy untested tools. Failed pushed rolls can erase files, corrupt systems, leave evidence, alert defenders, or infect the investigator's own system.

### Demolitions

Demolitions covers safe use, placement, removal, and defusing of explosive charges, including military explosives.

Special uses:

- Mines and similar devices are usually easy to set and do not require a roll.
- Removing or defusing explosives usually requires a roll.
- With enough time and materials, the skill can rig charges for demolition, clear obstructions, repurpose explosives, or make low-yield traps.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Defuse a known/common explosive; know where to place charges for structural effect | Regular |
| Defuse an unfamiliar or radically different explosive | Hard |

Pushing: work to the last second, check connections by hand, or research the device. Failed pushed defusal commonly causes detonation. Failed pushed placement can mis-time the blast, fail to detonate, or produce too much or too little effect.

### Disguise

Disguise presents the investigator as another person or a type of person through posture, costume, voice, makeup, and supporting props.

Special uses:

- Art/Craft (Acting) may grant a bonus die for performance.
- Art/Craft (Forgery) may grant a bonus die if fake documents support the disguise.
- Disguise can be opposed by Observation or Read Person.
- It can conceal true identity or impersonate someone else.
- Impersonating a specific person face to face with someone who knows them requires a Hard combined Art/Craft (Acting) and Disguise roll.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Convince strangers of the presented identity | Regular |
| Convince professionals in face-to-face scrutiny | Hard |

Pushing: deepen preparation, steal personal items, exaggerate the role, or create a distraction such as illness. Failed pushed rolls can cause arrest, offence, violence, or consequences from the identity being imitated.

### Diving

Diving covers scuba training, pressurised-air safety, diving equipment use and maintenance, emergency procedures, weighting, and underwater navigation. Normal diving conditions require no roll.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Dive in challenging conditions or with poorly maintained equipment | Regular |
| Dive in dangerous conditions or exceed depth/time limits | Hard |

Pushing: exceed equipment limits, re-check methodically, or call in professional help. Failed pushed rolls can trap the diver, cause decompression sickness, cause injury, or result in becoming lost underwater.

### History

History recalls facts about a country, city, region, person, period, tool, technique, idea, or event from the past.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Recall useful relevant historical information | Regular |
| Recall obscure, highly detailed, and useful information | Hard |

Pushing: research further, consult another expert, or hire help. Failed pushed rolls can alert opposition, introduce false facts, waste time, or waste money.

### Languages

Languages is always taken as a specialisation. Each specialisation covers understanding, speaking, reading, and writing a language.

Creation and acquisition:

- A generic Languages skill cannot be taken.
- The investigator's own language is free at INT or 60, whichever is higher.
- A bilingual second language is free at the same value if equally fluent, or at half INT or 30, whichever is higher, if less fluent.
- Selecting Languages as a skill gives one specialisation at 60 and two at 30.
- Languages can be selected more than once to add more languages.
- Sign language is a Languages specialisation.
- Language (Latin) is not required to take Magic or cast spells.

Use:

- A successful roll normally covers comprehension of an entire book, presentation, or discussion.
- Everyday texts and presentations in the investigator's own language do not require rolls.
- Archaic, technical, dialect-heavy, or unusually difficult material can require a roll even in the investigator's own language.
- If the needed language is absent but related to a known language, roll the lowest related specialisation with a penalty die.

Proficiency:

| Value | Language ability |
|---:|---|
| 30 | Basic transactional use. |
| 50 | Fluency. |
| 70 | Can pass for a native speaker. |

Identifying unknown languages:

- Present-day human language: INT roll.
- Extinct human language: History or Science (Archaeology).

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Fast conversation; written material with a few uncommon or technical terms | Regular |
| Dense technical material, archaic phrasing, or unusual dialect | Hard |

Pushing: take more time, pause before answering, or consult books/apps. Failed pushed rolls can reverse meaning, cause offence, alert opposition, or turn locals against the investigator.

Example language groups for related-language rulings:

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Romance | Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, French |
| Germanic | German, English, Dutch, Swedish |
| Afro-Asiatic | Egyptian, Hebrew, Arabic, Berber |
| Sino-Tibetan | Mandarin Chinese, Burmese, Xiang |
| Slavic | Russian, Polish, Czech, Croatian |
| Celtic | Irish, Welsh, Cornish, Gaelic |
| Niger-Congo | Swahili, Shona, Zulu |
| Indo-Iranian | Bengali, Hindi/Urdu, Pashto, Persian, Punjabi |
| Algonquian | Cheyenne, Cree, Ojibwe, Mohican |

### Law

Law covers a country's laws, precedents, legal procedure, courts, legal manoeuvres, and related authority. For police investigators, Law represents both legal knowledge and the professional authority attached to the occupation.

Foreign legal systems may increase difficulty unless the investigator has spent months studying that jurisdiction.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Understand or apply relevant law or procedure | Regular |
| Recall obscure precedent; cross-examine a highly competent hostile witness | Hard |

Pushing: delay to refine an argument, research more, explain procedural detail, or push the boundaries of law. Failed pushed rolls can break procedure, attract police scrutiny, waste legal resources, or lead to contempt of court.

### Locksmith

Locksmith opens and repairs locks, makes keys, uses picks and skeleton keys, opens car doors, hotwires cars, opens simple windows, solves puzzle boxes, and penetrates ordinary commercial alarm systems.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Open or repair a standard lock | Regular |
| Open a high-security lock | Hard |

Pushing: dismantle the lock, take longer, or force the mechanism. Failed pushed rolls can break tools, damage the lock beyond repair, trigger alarms, destroy the box, or make noise.

### Magic

Magic covers Newtonian spellcasting and detection of spellcasting. Full spellcasting, vestigia, signare, magic point, HTD, and spell rules belong in `09-magic.md` and `10-spells.md`.

Base constraints:

- A non-fae investigator must have the Magical advantage to select Magic.
- Starting Magic is 60.
- Each practitioner has a signare.
- Clear-headed casting matters; intoxication and recreational drugs make magic difficult.
- Spell use damages or destroys silicon chips.
- Human nervous systems resist magical control, making gross control effects very difficult.
- Overuse can cause Hyperthaumaturgical Degradation.
- Demi-monde magic does not use the Magic skill.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Cast a spell; sense that a spell is being cast | Regular |
| Sense that a spell is being cast and identify the spell or its likely effect | Hard |

Some spells are opposed by POW or CON as listed in their spell descriptions.

Pushing: take more time, intensify the forma, or force the casting through willpower. Failed pushed rolls can cause HTD or other spellcasting harm.

### Mechanical Repair

Mechanical Repair fixes or creates machines, uses basic carpentry and plumbing techniques, builds or repairs simple mechanical systems, and opens simple household locks.

Limits and interactions:

- Complex locks use Locksmith.
- Special parts or tools may be required.
- Tech and Mechanical Repair may both be required for complex devices such as cars or aircraft.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Repair or create a simple device; open a basic household lock | Regular |
| Repair a badly damaged device; create a complex device with limited resources | Hard |

Pushing: dismantle the device, take longer, or use risky force. Failed pushed rolls can break the device, injure the investigator, or consume excessive time.

### Medicine

Medicine diagnoses and treats illness, injury, disease, poisoning, drug effects, public health issues, contraindications, and side effects.

Medical emergency rules:

- Basic first aid for injury or illness requires no Medicine roll.
- Treating a mortal wound with first aid requires an INT roll under the damage rules.
- Medicine can save a character with a mortal wound.
- Medicine treatment for a mortal wound must occur within one hour unless first aid has been given.
- A modern, well-equipped hospital can grant automatic success for suitable treatment.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Diagnose and treat standard ailments with equipment and suitable environment | Regular |
| Diagnose or treat in a dirty or unsafe environment with minimal equipment | Hard |

Pushing: consult colleagues, research further, try experimental treatment, or perform a clinical experiment. Failed pushed rolls can worsen the patient, trigger investigation or malpractice consequences, or kill a dying patient.

### Occult

Occult covers secret knowledge, folk traditions, ritual concepts, occult paraphernalia, grimoires, occult codes, divination, and non-standard bodies of belief.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Recall occult theories, folklore, movement lore, or divination meanings | Regular |
| Identify rare tomes or recall secret ritual details | Hard |

Pushing: study longer, research, consult another expert, or enter deeper occult practice. Failed pushed rolls can recall information incorrectly, cause offence, or invert the meaning of signs.

### Pilot Aircraft

Pilot Aircraft covers fixed-wing aircraft, gliders, cargo planes, helicopters, instrument use, bad weather flying, low visibility, and emergency handling.

Special rules:

- Calm flight in good visibility with modest skill requires no roll.
- Storms, instrument navigation, low visibility, damage, and other hazards require a roll.
- Landing always requires a Pilot Aircraft roll.
- Failure when landing usually damages the aircraft, though consequences scale with conditions.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Land a light aircraft in a farmer's field during daylight | Regular |
| Make an emergency landing; control a faulty aircraft in flight | Hard |

Pushing: go around for one final landing attempt, push the aircraft to its limits, or make a risky pursuit manoeuvre. Failed pushed rolls can damage the aircraft, injure passengers, strand the group, or crash in severe cases.

### Pilot Boat

Pilot Boat covers motor craft and sailing craft from kayaks to large ships, including wind, storms, tides, wave action, hidden obstacles, and marine weather.

Special rules:

- Calm sailing in good visibility with modest skill requires no roll.
- Storms, instrument navigation, low visibility, damage, and other hazards require a roll.
- For simple craft such as canoes, rowboats, and kayaks, the GM may use Athletics if that better fits the character's actual experience.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Pilot across a strong tide or in poor conditions | Regular |
| Pilot with faulty equipment or in severe conditions | Hard |

Pushing: make a second docking attempt, push the craft to its limit, or attempt a risky manoeuvre. Failed pushed rolls can damage the craft, injure passengers, strand the group, or cause shipwreck.

### Read Lips

Read Lips interprets speech visually. Line of sight is required. If only one speaker's lips are visible, only that side of the conversation can be deciphered. Two proficient characters can communicate silently with each other.

Observation can be used with a penalty die for lip-reading if this expert skill is absent.

Read Lips is usually unopposed. If the target is trying to remain hidden or unobserved, Stealth can oppose it.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Understand a visible speaker at relatively close range | Regular |
| Understand a speaker whose mouth is partly obscured, distant, or in a crowd | Hard |

Pushing: take an obvious watching position or film the target. Failed pushed rolls can reveal the surveillance, provoke confrontation, misread the speech, or miss nearby danger.

### Ride

Ride covers ridden mounts (horses, donkeys, mules): looking after the animal, handling its tack, and keeping control at a gallop or over difficult ground.

Special rules:

- If a mount unexpectedly rears or stumbles, staying mounted is a Ride roll.
- An unfamiliar mount, such as a camel, can increase difficulty.
- Basic animal handling and riding at walking pace require no roll.
- Falling from a mount uses the other-damage rules, usually moderate or severe depending on ground and circumstances.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Ride at speed | Regular |
| Ride at speed in rough unfamiliar terrain; hold onto a scared mount | Hard |

Pushing: force the mount, attempt a dangerous jump, or stay mounted when jumping clear would be safer. Failed pushed rolls can throw the rider, injure the mount, or drag the rider.

### Science

Science is always taken as a specialisation. It covers formal or well-developed practical and theoretical knowledge in a discipline.

Creation rule:

- Choose one Science specialisation at 60.
- Choose two other Science specialisations at 30 each.
- Science may be selected more than once for more specialisations.

If the obvious science is absent, a related specialisation can be rolled at the lowest relevant specialisation with a penalty die if the GM agrees there is overlap.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Conduct an experiment, analyse an unusual substance, or know current theory in the field | Regular |
| Conduct a demanding experiment, read disorganised records, or work with improvised equipment or limited resources | Hard |

Pushing: study longer, research further, move to a better laboratory, consult an expert, or take risks. Failed pushed rolls can damage equipment, ruin rare material, cause hazardous effects, create a flawed formula, or lead authorities to seize results.

Common specialisations:

| Specialisation | Scope |
|---|---|
| Archaeology | Dating and identifying artefacts, excavations, site interpretation, detecting ancient fakes, identifying extinct written languages. |
| Astronomy | Stars, planets, eclipses, meteor showers, celestial navigation, orbits, stellar theory, and observational techniques. |
| Biology | Life science, cytology, ecology, genetics, histology, microbiology, physiology, blood and organic analysis. |
| Botany | Plant classification, structure, growth, reproduction, chemistry, uses, toxicity, edibility, and psychotropic properties. |
| Chemistry | Substance composition, reactions, extraction, compounds, acids, gases, poisons, simple explosives, and unknown-substance analysis. |
| Cryptography | Codes, hidden messages, code creation, code identification, and codebreaking. |
| Engineering | Practical application of scientific principles to machines, structures, and materials. |
| Forensics | Evidence analysis, crime-scene material, fingerprints, DNA, hair, body fluids, and expert evidence. |
| Geology | Rock strata, fossils, minerals, crystals, drilling/mining sites, soil, volcanoes, seismic events, and avalanches. |
| Mathematics | Number, logic, theory, formulas, unusual geometries, patterns, and code-adjacent analysis. |
| Meteorology | Weather systems, atmospheric phenomena, long-range weather patterns, rain, snow, and fog. |
| Pharmacy | Pharmaceutical compounds, dispensing, synthesis, toxins, side effects, and safe drug use. |
| Physics | Pressure, materials, motion, magnetism, electricity, optics, radioactivity, and experimental devices. |
| Zoology | Animal biology, classification, behaviour, tracks, droppings, marks, territory, and species identification. |

### Sleight of Hand

Sleight of Hand covers palming, pickpocketing, concealing objects, masking items with debris or cloth, false compartments, covert phone use, and other fine manipulation.

Characters without Sleight of Hand may attempt simple feats with a Hard DEX roll.

Observation commonly opposes Sleight of Hand. Larger concealed objects increase difficulty.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Pick a pocket against Observation below 70; conceal a small pocket-sized item | Regular |
| Pick a pocket against Observation 70+; remove a watch or belt unnoticed; conceal a larger item | Hard |

Pushing: study the target longer, bump or touch the target, build a concealment rig, or use a distraction. Failed pushed rolls can cause discovery, suspicion, pursuit, police action, or immediate retaliation.

### Survival

Survival covers staying alive in deserts, arctic conditions, at sea, and in wilderness; hunting; shelter; finding water; finding food; and avoiding environmental hazards.

Navigate is still required to find the way through terrain. Survival keeps the investigator alive long enough to be rescued or find help.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Find or make shelter, locate food/water, or notice an environmental hazard | Regular |
| Do the same while badly injured or in severe weather | Hard |

Pushing: drink unpurified water, eat unidentified food, sacrifice clothing or gear for shelter, or burn possessions for a signal. Failed pushed rolls can cause poisoning, exposure, injury, discovery by pursuers, or entry into a dangerous location.

### Tech

Tech repairs and reconfigures technological and electronic devices: mobile phones, computers, electric motors, fuse boxes, burglar alarms, circuits, and similar equipment.

Special rules:

- Proper parts and tools may be required.
- With correct parts, instructions, and no time pressure, standard assembly or repair may not need a roll.
- Difficulty can be reduced or success made automatic when time is unlimited and manuals/parts are available.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Repair minor damage to a computer or electronic device | Regular |
| Repair badly damaged technology, work without proper tools, or rush under tight time | Hard |

Pushing: spend longer, take a risky shortcut, or buy expensive replacement parts. Failed pushed rolls can blow fuses, destroy the device, or create a device that behaves incorrectly.

### Track

Track follows a person, vehicle, or animal over earth and vegetation. Time since passage, rain, ground, terrain, and concealment affect difficulty.

If the target concealed tracks, Stealth can set the difficulty; terrain modifiers may still apply.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Track an animal or human in temperate forest under normal conditions | Regular |
| Follow a trail several days old or older | Hard |

Pushing: backtrack, study longer, search in expanding spirals, or accept more exposure and noise. Failed pushed rolls can lead into danger, waste daylight, get the investigator lost, or trigger an ambush or trap.

### Anthropology (Advanced option)

Anthropology is a sample new expert skill (see Custom Skills below). It identifies and understands an individual or culture's way of life through observation and study.

- After a month or more observing a culture or studying accurate records, make simple predictions about behaviour, values, and likely responses.
- Combined with Read Person, may help predict the actions and beliefs of people from the studied culture.

| Use | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Predicting behaviour after a month or more of study | Regular |
| Predicting behaviour after a week or less of study | Hard |

Pushing: spend more time studying, immerse fully in the culture, or take part in a rite, ceremony, or social practice. Failed pushed rolls can cause social transgression, hostility from the studied group, or harmful effects from a risky ceremony or substance.

## Custom Skills (Advanced option)

Before creating a new expert skill, check whether an existing skill can be adjusted. If not, define:

- What the skill does.
- Whether another skill opposes it.
- What counts as Regular and Hard difficulty.
- How the roll can be pushed.

## Combat Skills

Combat skills begin at 30 and may be selected as starting skills at 60. Combat skills cannot be pushed.

### Fighting

Fighting covers melee combat, unarmed attacks, instinctive melee defence, and melee weapons such as knives, clubs, batons, improvised weapons, and similar weapons.

The rules do not differentiate between fighting styles or melee weapon styles. A high starting value can be justified by martial arts, boxing, military training, personal security training, street fighting, or similar background.

Signature Weapon grants a bonus die on damage rolls only with the named melee weapon.

### Firearms

Firearms covers handguns, rifles, shotguns, derivative firearms, bows, and crossbows.

The rules do not differentiate between firearm or bow types at the skill level. A high starting value can be justified by military training, armed police training, sport shooting, archery, farm upbringing, criminal background, or another suitable explanation.

Signature Firearm grants a bonus die on damage rolls only with the named firearm or bow.
