# Advancement

<!-- Source: core-rules, rules-summaries. Status: reviewed-complete. -->

## Development Phase

Run an investigator development phase at the end of a case file or campaign chapter. If a case or chapter runs longer than roughly three or four sessions, the GM should look for a natural pause and run a development phase there.

During the phase, handle these items:

- Spend development points.
- Review employment and Affluence Rating if events have changed the investigator's situation.
- Alter or add backstory entries, with GM approval.
- Handle training if there is enough downtime.
- Recover from impairment where the relevant recovery rule allows it.

## Gaining Development Points

At the end of each case file or campaign chapter, each investigator gains development points.

Default award:

- `1` development point per play session.

Pre-written case files may specify their own awards.

The GM may adjust awards to fit the campaign pace. Higher awards produce faster skill, spell, and advantage growth. Lower awards produce slower advancement. A group that plays rarely may want extra points at case end; a group that plays very often may want only `1` point per case file rather than per session.

Development points are spent only during an investigator development phase. Unspent points may be saved.

## Spending Development Points

Development points can be spent on:

- Increasing existing skills.
- Buying a new skill.
- Learning a spell.
- Mastering a spell.
- Buying an advantage.

## Increasing Skills

During one development phase, each skill can be raised only once.

| Current skill value | Cost | Increase | Maximum after increase |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Below 80 | 1 development point | +10 to one skill or +5 to two skills | 80 |
| 80 to 89 | 1 development point | +5 to one skill | 90 |
| 90 or higher | 1 development point | +1 to one skill | 99 |

The GM may ignore the one-step-per-skill limit if the campaign wants faster or less novel-like advancement, but doing so lets investigators become highly specialised quickly.

## Buying a New Skill

An investigator may spend `1` development point to gain a new skill.

The new skill starts at half of its linked characteristic. Use the linked characteristic table in `02-core-resolution.md`.

## Learning and Mastering Spells

Only practitioner investigators can spend development points on spells.

During one development phase, a practitioner may:

- Spend `1` development point to learn one new spell.
- Spend `1` development point to master one known spell.

Limits:

- A practitioner can learn at most one new spell per development phase.
- A practitioner can master at most one spell per development phase.
- With `2` development points, a practitioner may learn one spell and master one spell.
- Extra points do not permit learning multiple new spells or mastering multiple spells in the same phase.

Full spell learning and mastery rules are in `09-magic.md` and `10-spells.md`.

## Buying Advantages

An investigator may buy a new advantage during a development phase.

| Advantage type | Cost |
|---|---:|
| Regular advantage | 5 development points |
| Major advantage | 10 development points |

Advantages are significant benefits. They are intentionally much slower to acquire than small skill increases.

## Backstory Changes

During a development phase, a player may alter or add character backstory entries when case events justify it.

Requirements:

- Discuss the change with the GM.
- The GM may veto changes that would derail the game or contradict established events.
- The GM may suggest backstory changes prompted by what happened during the case.

## Employment and Affluence Review

If the story significantly changes an investigator's employment, income, resources, or social position, review their Affluence Rating.

Affluence bands are broad, so changes should be uncommon. Move a character up or down only when events clearly justify it and the GM approves.

## Training

If there is a sufficient break between case files, usually several weeks or more, an investigator may receive training.

Training can justify a development point award outside the normal case award, but the recommended maximum is:

- `1` development point per investigator for training between case files.

This limit applies whether the downtime is weeks or years.
