# Damage and Healing

<!-- Advanced corpus: derived from rules/07-damage-and-healing.md. Sections labelled "(Advanced option)" are optional and require GM permission. -->

## Damage Roll

Make a damage roll after a successful attack if the winning goal was to inflict harm.

| Attack type | Damage roll |
|---|---|
| Unarmed melee | STR |
| Melee weapon | STR |
| Firearm or other ranged attack | DEX |
| Spell attack that causes damage | DEX |

Missed attacks cause no damage.

Damage roll result:

| Damage roll result | Damage caused |
|---|---|
| Fumble or failure | 1 damage; do not add base weapon or spell damage |
| Regular success | 1 damage + base weapon or spell damage |
| Hard success | 2 damage + base weapon or spell damage |
| Critical success | 3 damage + base weapon or spell damage |

For firearms base damage and ranges, see `06-combat.md`. For melee weapon base damage, see `03-character-creation.md`. For spell base damage, see `10-spells.md`.

## Damage Roll Modifiers

Apply any relevant modifiers:

- A critical success on the attack roll, including one bought with Luck, gives one bonus die on the damage roll.
- If the attack roll had two or more net bonus dice after cancellation, a successful attack gives one bonus die on the damage roll.
- If the attack roll had two or more net penalty dice after cancellation, a successful attack gives one penalty die on the damage roll.
- Damage Bonus adds `+1` damage to unarmed and melee attacks only.
- Natural Toughness, armour, cover, and some spells can reduce damage.
- Cold iron can affect some fae; see `11-demi-monde.md`.

Spending Luck on a damage roll improves the roll's success level. It does not add damage point-for-point.

## Damage Rolls: Alternative Method (Advanced option)

This optional method replaces the standard damage roll with relative success levels.

For opposed attacks:

| Result | Damage |
| --- | --- |
| Same success level, attacker wins because it is their turn | 1 damage; do not add base weapon damage. |
| Attacker wins by 1 success level | 1 damage plus base weapon damage. |
| Attacker wins by 2 success levels | 2 damage plus base weapon damage. |
| Attacker wins by 3 success levels | 3 damage plus base weapon damage. |
| Attacker wins by 4 success levels | 4 damage plus base weapon damage. |

For unopposed attacks:

| Result | Damage |
| --- | --- |
| Attacker achieves exactly the required success level | 1 damage; do not add base weapon damage. |
| Attacker achieves 1 success level above the requirement | 1 damage plus base weapon damage. |
| Attacker achieves 2 success levels above the requirement | 2 damage plus base weapon damage. |

Examples of opposed margins:

| Margin | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| 1 level | Weak vs fumble; Regular vs Weak; Hard vs Regular; Critical vs Hard. |
| 2 levels | Regular vs fumble; Hard vs Weak; Critical vs Regular. |
| 3 levels | Hard vs fumble; Critical vs Weak. |
| 4 levels | Critical vs fumble. |

As normal, 4 damage in one blow is a mortal wound, and 5 damage in one attack is a fatal blow.

## Spell Damage

Spell attacks use the combat procedure in `06-combat.md`, replacing Firearms with Magic for the attack roll.

If the spell hits and causes damage:

1. Roll DEX for damage.
2. Apply the damage roll result.
3. Add the spell's base damage, if any.

Non-damaging spells apply their own effect instead of a damage roll.

## Damage Conditions

Damage in a fight is cumulative. Mark every damage box up to the current condition.

| Damage suffered | Condition | During the fight |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 total | Hurt | Painful but no mechanical penalty. |
| 2 total | Bloodied | Impaired. |
| 3 or more total | Down | Incapacitated and impaired. |
| 4 in a single blow | Mortal wound | Down and dying without urgent aid. |
| 5 or more in a single attack | Fatal blow | Investigator must spend 30 Luck or die outright. |

Only investigators can spend Luck to reduce a fatal blow. Spending 30 Luck reduces the damage to 4, creating a mortal wound.

## Hurt

The character has a painful blow, bruise, graze, or flesh wound. They can continue acting normally.

## Bloodied

The character is still active but badly hurt.

Effects:

- The character is impaired.
- Any skill or characteristic roll of 90 or higher is a fumble.
- If the character was already impaired, there is no additional mechanical effect.

## Down

The character is floored and out of the fight. They are not automatically dead or unconscious, but they cannot take further actions in that fight.

Further incidental damage to a downed character is ignored. A deliberate attempt to murder a downed character normally succeeds if the attacker is able to carry it out.

<!-- Advanced: Spending Luck to Resist Incapacitation | supplement -->
*Advanced option (Spending Luck to Resist Incapacitation):* a character who suffers 3+ damage in combat and would be Down may instead spend Luck to remain active briefly.

| Cost | Effect |
| ---: | --- |
| 5 Luck | Remain active until the end of the current round. |
| 5 additional Luck | Remain active for one more round; pay at the start of that round. |

- If the character takes further damage, they are immediately Down and can no longer resist incapacitation.
- A character with a Mortal Wound may use the same Luck spend, but the GM decides what actions are still physically possible.
- The Slow Healer disadvantage forbids this Luck spend.

## Death

A character who suffers 5 or more damage from one attack takes a fatal blow.

- An investigator may immediately spend 30 Luck to reduce the fatal blow to a mortal wound.
- If the investigator cannot or will not spend 30 Luck, they die.
- NPCs and creatures use GM judgement unless the GM wants to apply the same rules.

## After the Fight

When combat ends, stop using combat rounds and assess each injured character.

## After the Fight: Hurt

Hurt clears soon after the fight.

For an investigator, erase the Hurt check.

## After the Fight: Bloodied

Bloodied clears soon after the fight, though the character may visibly look injured.

For an investigator:

- Erase Hurt and Bloodied.
- If impairment came only from being bloodied, erase Impaired.
- If impairment came from another cause, recover by that cause's normal rule.

## After the Fight: Down

A Down character partly recovers after the fight.

For an investigator:

- Erase Bloodied and Down.
- Remain Hurt until the end of the following day, or spend 10 Luck to clear Hurt.
- Remain impaired until the end of the following day, or spend 10 Luck to clear Impaired.

Spending 20 Luck clears both the lingering Hurt and impairment.

For an NPC, the GM may apply these rules or decide the outcome by story need.

## Mortal Wounds

A character suffers a mortal wound when they take exactly 4 damage from one blow or when a fatal blow is reduced by 30 Luck.

Effects:

- The character is Down.
- The character cannot act.
- Without urgent professional treatment or hospitalisation, the character dies within one hour.
- Successful first aid can extend that time to two hours.

## First Aid

Any character can attempt basic first aid for a mortal wound.

Roll INT.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Success | Time before death extends from within one hour to within two hours. |
| Failure or fumble | No benefit. |

If first aid succeeds before a Luck roll is made to see whether an ambulance or medical professional arrives in time, that Luck roll gains one bonus die.

Luck can be spent on the first-aid INT roll. The roll can be pushed, but failure means the dying character's time has not been extended.

## Medical Aid

Only Medicine can save a character with a mortal wound, unless hospital treatment is reached in time.

Requirements:

- The helper has the Medicine skill.
- Suitable medical equipment is available. A doctor, paramedic, or nurse's bag is the minimum.

Medicine roll modifiers:

| Situation | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Single-handed treatment in the street with minimum equipment | One penalty die |
| Safe, clean place with reasonable equipment and supplies | No modifier |
| Assistant plus good facilities and equipment | One bonus die |
| Well-equipped modern hospital | No roll; the character is saved |

If the Medicine roll fails, the character dies unless Luck is spent to make it succeed. A pushed Medicine roll is possible only if the players describe a plausible changed approach.

If no one can provide Medicine, rushing the patient to hospital may be enough. If access to an ambulance, paramedic, or hospital is uncertain, the GM may call for a Luck roll for the dying character. Failure means help arrives too late.

## Recovering from a Mortal Wound

If medical aid saves an investigator from a mortal wound, the player chooses two consequences.

| Consequence | Effect |
|---|---|
| Injured | Bloodied for three days, then Hurt for the remaining four days of the week. The investigator is impaired for the whole duration. |
| Luck Loss | Lose 20 Luck. |
| Development Loss | Lose 1 development point. |
| Disfigurement | Gain a significant, lasting physical change. This can be chosen only once per investigator. |

Development Loss:

- If the investigator has saved development points, lose one immediately.
- If not, the GM may allow the loss to be deferred until the end of the case file.
- A deferred loss can happen only once per case file.
- A deferred loss may reduce the next development award to zero.

Disfigurement should be significant and visible or otherwise life-changing. It is a feature of the investigator, not a mechanical penalty.

## Recovering from Impairment

Impairment has different recovery rules depending on its cause.

Mental trauma impairment clears when any one of the following happens:

- The case file ends.
- The investigator spends 10 Luck.
- The investigator takes a break from the case for three or more days and succeeds at a Regular POW roll. Therapy or counselling gives one bonus die to that POW roll.

Physical impairment clears as follows:

| Cause | Recovery |
|---|---|
| Bloodied | When the fight ends. |
| Down | End of the following day, or spend 10 Luck. |
| Mortal wound with Injured consequence | When the Injured consequence fully ends. |

An investigator is either impaired or not. Multiple causes do not stack, but each unresolved cause may matter for when impairment can clear.

## Other Forms of Damage

Use this table for environmental harm, accidents, falls, drowning, fire, explosions, poison, collisions, and other non-attack damage.

Each listed damage instance applies to one incident or one round of exposure. If the source continues, apply it again each round or interval.

Anything below Moderate can often be handled narratively with no lasting mechanical effect.

| Level | Suggested damage | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | CON roll; on failure take 1 damage, otherwise no damage | Mild acid, smoky air, thrown fist-sized rock, 3 metre/yard fall onto soft swamp |
| Moderate | 1 damage | 3 metre/yard fall onto grass, strong acid, breathing water, exposure to vacuum, burning torch |
| Severe | 2 damage | 3 metre/yard fall onto concrete, intense fire, 6 to 9 metres/yards from a hand grenade or dynamite blast, mild poison |
| Deadly | 3 damage | Hit by a car at about 48 kph / 30 mph, 3 to 6 metres/yards from a hand grenade or dynamite blast, strong poison |
| Terminal | 4 damage, probably a mortal wound | Hit by a vehicle at 65+ kph / 40+ mph, within 3 metres/yards of a hand grenade or dynamite blast, lethal poison |
| Splat | CON roll; on success suffer a mortal wound, on failure die immediately unless 30 Luck reduces it to a mortal wound | High-speed head-on collision, hit by a train |

## Asphyxiation and Drowning

When a character is asphyxiating or drowning:

- Make a CON roll at the start of each turn in combat, or each minute outside combat.
- Each failed CON roll causes 1 damage while the threat remains.
- For Moderate water or vacuum exposure, apply one penalty die to the CON roll after the third round or third minute.

## Poison

Poison always calls for a CON roll.

| CON result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Failure | Apply the poison's listed damage level. |
| Regular success | Move the poison one level less severe on the other-damage table. |
| Hard success | Move the poison two levels less severe. |
| Critical success | GM may allow the poison to be shaken off completely. |

Poison may also cause symptoms such as stomach pain, vomiting, diarrhoea, chills, sweats, cramps, jaundice, altered heart rate, impaired vision, convulsions, unconsciousness, or paralysis.

The GM decides whether symptoms prevent action, impose a penalty die, or increase difficulty.

## Detailed Poisons (Advanced option)

Use this optional system instead of the quick poison rule above when a specific poison matters.

Every poison has a type (very mild, mild, strong, or lethal), a speed, and a one-dose effect.

- Multiple doses add one penalty die to the victim's CON roll to resist.
- The GM decides whether symptoms prevent action, allow action with a penalty die, or increase difficulty.
- When poisoned, the victim makes a CON roll.

| CON result | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Failure | Apply the poison's listed severity. |
| Regular success | Reduce severity by one level. |
| Hard success | Reduce severity by two levels. |
| Critical success | GM may allow the poison to be shaken off entirely. |

If the CON roll fails, the type sets the effect:

| Poison type | Damage effect |
| --- | --- |
| Very mild | No damage; temporary unconsciousness for 1D6 minutes or rounds, as appropriate. |
| Mild | 2 damage; recovery takes about 24 hours. |
| Strong | 4 damage, causing a Mortal Wound. |
| Lethal | Make another CON roll. Failure means immediate death; success means a Mortal Wound. |

Fast poisons may take effect within a single round. Slower poisons may take hours or days.
