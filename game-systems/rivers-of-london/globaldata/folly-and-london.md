# The Folly And London

This file is a scenario-facing gazetteer of the Folly, the wider world of English
magic, and the parts of London that see the most Falcon (supernatural) activity.
It is a GM reference for running and writing cases, not an app-facing rules file.
The default era is around `2016`. "Falcon" is the Met's call-name for anything in
the Folly's remit; the Folly's radio call sign is `Zulu Foxtrot`.

## The Folly And English Magic

The Folly is the official home of English magic, established `1775`, and the body
that has kept the Queen's Peace in the supernatural realm since before the
Metropolitan Police existed. Officially it is the Met's **Special Assessment Unit
(SAU)** (previously "Economic and Specialist Crime Unit 9"). Its head, **DCI
Thomas Nightingale**, answers only to the Commissioner and, beyond them, the Home
Secretary. Magic is not officially acknowledged, and casting spells is not
illegal.

### A short history of magic

- Magic is as old as humanity - shamans, family witch-traditions, trade-bound
  craft groups such as the **Sons of Weyland**, and alchemists.
- In the `17th` century **Sir Isaac Newton** worked out how magic could be done
  safely. His second *Principia* (*Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Artes Magicis*)
  set out the Newtonian forms still taught today. An unpublished third book on
  alchemy was stolen in `1719`.
- The discipline is taught and policed by the **Society of the Wise**.

### The Society of the Wise

- Newtonian practitioners first met on a floating coffee house called "The Folly,"
  moored by Somerset House - a "dodgy secret society" with royal favour.
- On moving into a folly tower on the Bedford estate they voted to become
  men-only. Fellows use the post-nominal **FSW** (Fellow of the Society of the
  Wise). **Sir Victor Casterbrook** was the first president; only he and Newton
  have busts in the atrium.
- Late `18th` century: the estate was sold and **Russell Square** created; the
  Society took a new clubhouse on the south terrace but kept the old name.
- The Folly was historically half gentlemen's club, half unofficial arm of the
  establishment. Volunteer wizards were sworn in as constables (listed at Scotland
  Yard as inspectors, paid by the Folly). Many such **agreements** still shape the
  work - awkward, but necessary when dealing with deities and the demi-monde.
- At its `19th`/early-`20th`-century height the Society claimed sole authority to
  licence UK magic; in practice it ignored those (often women) who were no
  trouble. **WWII and Ettersberg** gutted it, leaving Nightingale as Britain's
  last official wizard. In `1953` he formalised the Folly's place in the Met as a
  Detective Inspector; the building is leased as a police station.
- Magic did **not** in fact die out, so the Folly is now expanding and recruiting
  under a sanctioned "Folly Expansion" - the in-world reason for new investigators.
  Identified non-criminal practitioners are asked to swear an oath and train for
  safety (botched magic causes **Hyperthaumaturgical Degradation**).

### Practitioners outside the Folly

- **County Practitioners (CPs):** pre-war, each county had an official watcher for
  supernatural problems, filing annual records that often went un-actioned. Quality
  varied wildly; some pursued unethical "pet projects." Old CP files may surface
  feral legacies.
- **Hedge witches / wizards:** practitioners without Folly training, or retired
  ("rusticated") Fellows. Some learned a trick from family; some develop ability
  spontaneously, sometimes tied to a neurological condition. The Folly keeps paper
  files (manila folders) on rusticated Fellows - a recruit may be tasked with
  digitising them into DC Grant's new database.

### Allied institutions and the school

- **The Special Collection:** the Folly's rarest books live underground at
  Oxford's Bodleian (Radcliffe Science Library), curated by archivist **Dr Harold
  Postmartin**, who also keeps the unwritten agreements that make up English
  magical law. Access is swipe-card secured; the index runs on a deliberately
  obsolete stand-alone computer.
- **Casterbrook:** the former boarding school (Ambrose Estate, Buckinghamshire)
  that trained Fellows. Requisitioned in WWII and never reopened; the Folly owns
  the estate (a healthy operational budget comes from its rents). The school hall
  carries the carved names of wizards who died serving, many at Ettersberg.
- **Sons of Weyland:** a northern tradition of binding magic into metal (smithing)
  and horse-lore, tied to the old horse-fairs. They train only those who earn it;
  Nightingale is one.
- **Ettersberg:** in the last days of WWII, British wizards raided a Nazi bunker
  near Ettersberg to seize horrific magical research (the "Black Library"). They
  succeeded, but three in five British wizards of military age died - `2,396`
  people. The catastrophe is why magic was thought a spent force.

### Other magical organisations

- **Little Crocodiles:** a secret Oxford dining club where Professor Geoffrey
  Wheatcroft taught magic in the `1950s`. Most students learned little; one,
  **Albert Woodville-Gentle**, became the original masked "**Faceless Man**" and
  trained a successor (**Faceless Man 2**) - a recurring criminal-practitioner
  threat.
- **La Societe de la Rose** (Society of the Rose): a women's tradition descended
  from Queen Caroline's court, taught mother to daughter, specialising in healing.
  Current members **Lady Helena** and her daughter **Lady Caroline** are on good
  terms with the Folly; Lady Caroline prefers "witch" or "sorceress."
- **The Paternoster Society:** poses as a historical society funding digs
  (London/Arthurian interest); privately a group of unofficial practitioners,
  some Oxford-trained. They rent rooms near St Paul's fitted out in imitation of
  the Folly. Tracing their members is on the Folly's action list.

## The Folly Building

Above the door: *Scientia Potestas Est* ("knowledge is power"). **No computers**
anywhere in the main building - magic "sands" microprocessors (the basement
custody suite is the partial exception). Valve electronics are fine. The building
is warded.

- **Ground floor:** entrance lobby; the old visitor's lounge **(1)** is now the
  briefing room; the grand **atrium (7)** with domed glass roof and Newton's bust;
  the mundane **library (6)** with a card index (and a concealed door to the
  mezzanine); dining room **(13)**, lecture hall **(11)**, old smoking rooms. Three
  atrium wall-panels hide narrow servants' corridors.
- **Basement:** Molly's domain (kitchens, kept off the plans for privacy); a modern
  **PACE-compliant custody suite** with six cells, search/medical/interview rooms;
  **Foxglove's art studio** doubles as a **Magical Suppression Area (MSA)** that
  stops cells' occupants casting. A maze of disused brick corridors leads to
  **Secure Storage** (the Black Library, the Mary Engine, and other dangerous
  items) - out of bounds without Nightingale's express permission. Lesser
  storerooms use magical dampening (cork, greenwood, Faraday cages); one holds the
  semi-sentient **Book of Cunning Device**.
- **Rear mews & coach house (15):** vehicle entrance off Bedford Place **(17)**;
  garage for the pool car and Nightingale's Jag (and a notoriously haunted second
  car - off limits). The coach-house attic is the **"tech cave"** - outside the
  no-tech zone, so laptops, a HOLMES 2 terminal, Airwave handsets, telly and beer
  live up there.
- **Mezzanine & first floor:** labs **(28)** and classrooms; the **magical library
  (18)** in glass-fronted cases; reading rooms, records office, billiard room.
- **Second floor:** old practitioners' rooms (mostly unused; some locked), a
  refectory, and a **smithy (32)** with forge and anvil.
- **Third floor:** the **Master's suite (44)** and office; a senior lounge; a
  glass-roofed arboretum.
- **Attic:** junior/servant bedrooms (unused since `1945`) and a small
  **observatory (59)**.
- **Teatime:** Molly serves tea and cakes in the atrium at break - everything stops
  for it.

## Police Stations And Facilities

(Bracketed numbers refer to the rulebook's Map of Central London.)

- **Charing Cross (CX) (4):** where Grant and May were probationers; polices Covent
  Garden.
- **Belgravia (AB) (5):** the **Westminster Murder Team** (DCI Alexander Seawoll, DI
  Miriam Stephanopoulos). Two custody cells are enchanted (iron inlaid with copper,
  hidden under paint). DS Sahra Guleed moved from here to the Folly.
- **Kentish Town (EK):** contact **DC Karen "Kay" Jonquiere**, trained in
  interviewing children/teens; Abigail Kamara has most dealt with her.
- **Walworth (MS) / Southwark (MD):** south of the river; contact **Sgt William
  Daverc**.
- **Bromley (PY) / Dulwich:** Bromley MIT under **DCI Maureen Duffy**; Dulwich hosts
  the Operational Technology Support Unit.
- **New Scotland Yard (6):** the **Commissioner** (fully briefed on the Folly; their
  approval is needed to be sworn into the Society). **DAC Richard Folsom** is an
  acolyte of **Lady Ty** and presses for more control over Folly matters - handle
  with diplomacy.
- **Empress State Building (ESB):** Met offices; high-level case meetings;
  Counter Terrorism Command and the Directorate of Professional Standards.
- **National Crime Agency (NCA):** contact via Officer **Alona Silver**.
- **British Transport Police (BTP):** London HQ in Camden; unofficial Falcon liaison
  **Sgt Jaget Kumar** (the Underground has more than its share of weirdness).
  Track-access escort via patrolman **David Lambert**.
- **Iain West Forensic Suite (13):** Westminster Public Mortuary; **Dr Abdul Haqq
  Walid** and **Dr Jennifer Vaughan** handle "special"/cryptopathology cases (more
  usually found at University College Hospital).

## A London Falcon Gazetteer

- **Bloomsbury:** home of the Folly; the British Museum (a magnet for magical
  items - and the odd one that wanders off), UCL with the **Harry Price Library**,
  the British Library at St Pancras, Great Ormond Street Hospital. Bloomsbury sits
  just **outside** Westminster, limiting Lady Ty's influence over the Folly.
- **Covent Garden (1):** a Falcon hotspot - theatres (the Royal Opera House),
  St Paul's "Actors' Church," and ghosts (including a Bow Street magistrate who can
  grant a "ghost warrant"). The bookshop case (*The Bookshop*) is set near here.
- **Soho (41):** former vice district, now media bars and clubs with a seedy
  underside. Sites tied to the Faceless Men, an unusual historic vampire nest at
  the Cafe de Paris, and the **Pale Lady** (a dangerous fae). Vampire nests are
  normally burned out via Fire Brigade liaison **Frank Caffrey**.
- **Leicester Square (39) & Chinatown (40):** West End entertainment; the
  long-empty Trocadero. **Michael Cheung**, the "Legendary Swordsman," represents
  Chinatown, which keeps its own peace under agreement with the Folly (meetings at
  Wong Kei on Wardour Street).
- **Charing Cross Road:** the book-trade quarter; esoteric/first-edition shops
  (Cecil Court, Watkins, Atlantis, Treadwell's) that would recognise - and report -
  a Newtonian tome.
- **Skygarden (18) / Elephant and Castle (42):** the demolished Skygarden Tower was
  built as an industrial magic "city crown" (*Stadtkrone*) powered by residents'
  vestigia. The crater is now a vestigia hot spot and an eco-protest site (inspired
  by Nicky, goddess of the Neckinger). Its architect, **Erik Stromberg** (a German
  practitioner), may have left secrets at his Highgate house.
- **Notting Hill:** carnival, wealth, and Portobello Road's antique market - where
  genuinely magical items occasionally surface (e.g. Quiet People "Unbreakable
  Pottery"). Demi-monde go-between **Zachary Palmer** operates here.
- **Underground London:** tunnels, sewers, GPO mail rail, WWII shelters (one is a
  demi-monde nightclub at Holland Park), and the hidden "ways" used by the **Quiet
  People** (an underground community who raise pigs and make Unbreakable Pottery).
  The Folly knows only a fraction of these routes. Underground access needs BTP
  permission (Sgt Kumar); sewer access via Thames Water contact **Allison Conte**.
- **City of London ("the Square Mile"):** its own police force (Folly liaison **SC
  Genevieve Nguyen**); Roman remains including the **London Mithraeum**; sites of
  unregulated practitioners (e.g. **Patrick Gale**, now on an agreement basis).
- **Smithfield:** a millennium-old meat market and former execution ground. Mass,
  violent death can spawn **revenants**; the Sons of Weyland's **Pacificatus**
  ritual disperses such energy (one installation is capped by a bronze statue of
  Peace). The Rising Sun pub sits on the demi-monde's fringe.
- **Newgate / Old Bailey:** a historic site of death and suffering; legends of a
  spectral black dog.
- **Old Street ("Silicon Roundabout"):** tech start-ups; **Serious Cybernetics
  Corporation** and the dangerous **Mary Engine** (now stored at the Folly).
- **Hampstead:** a wealthy hilltop bordering the Heath, between two branches of the
  Fleet. A monitored derelict house safely drains a dangerous genius loci. Contacts
  include a den of **intelligent talking foxes** (own agenda) under Gospel Oak
  Station, and the observant "Cat Lady."
- **Greater London:** vampire nests are likelier in a `1950s` semi than a Soho
  club. **Bromley** (outer borough; the Ravensbourne river) and **Epping Forest**
  (ancient woodland managed by City of London Police; reported **unicorn** sightings
  - fae, and dangerous).

## The Rivers Of London

The Thames and her tributaries carry powerful **genii locorum**. Many are as alive
and lawbreaking as Londoners; keeping the peace among them is core Falcon work. The
rivers always expect something big in return for a favour.

- **The Thames (27):** two powers - **Father Thames** (the elder, now rules above
  Teddington Lock) and **Mama Thames** (acting goddess of the river in London,
  living in converted warehouse flats near the Tower; bring an offering). Currently
  on good terms; keeping it that way is a Falcon matter. Of note along the river:
  the troll **Nathaniel** at Richmond Bridge; **Kew Gardens** for weird flora (the
  staff are wary of the Folly); the Court of the Thames' veiled **Spring Festival**
  on the South Bank; the **Thames River Police** at Wapping.
- **The Tyburn (21):** runs Hampstead to Pimlico, almost entirely underground.
  **Lady Ty** courts power and money, with houses in Hampstead and Mayfair; her
  daughter **Olivia** calls the route a "posh line" (St John's Wood, Mayfair,
  Knightsbridge, St James's). The river underlies Parliament. Avoid the Shepherd's
  Well fountain in her garden.
- **The Fleet (22):** shares a Hampstead source with the Tyburn; runs to the Thames
  at Blackfriars. The **Vale of Health** showmen propitiate the goddess each
  midsummer (with darker midwinter legends of the "Hatchett Man"). The goddess
  holds court on the Heath; her children attended **Acland Burghley** school by the
  Peckwater Estate (railway tunnel haunted by the ghost **Macky**).
- **The Walbrook (23):** eastern City, underground since the `15th` century.
  Goddess **Lulu** runs the demi-monde pub **The Goat and Crocodile** in Shoreditch.
- **The Effra (20):** Norwood to Vauxhall via **Brixton** (a large Afro-Caribbean
  community, herb shops, clubs); the goddess loves dance music.
- **Beverley Brook:** rises in Worcester Park, joins the Thames at Barn Elms;
  **Ms Brook** is now DC Grant's partner (note conflicts of interest).
- **The Wandle:** Croydon to Battersea; goddess **Wanda** (raised in the fashion
  trade) runs a boutique, "The Shop," that shifts premises but stays near the river.
- **Other tributaries:** the **Westbourne** (Chelsea), **Counter's Creek**
  (Olympia), the **Brent** and the **Lea**.
- **Grand Union / Regent's Canal:** spirit "**Mrs Canal**," an escaped orangutan,
  living near Regent's Park; appreciates bananas and greener living, and often
  knows the park's business better than Lady Ty.

## Demi-Monde Pubs And Nazareths

- **Demi-monde pubs** serve practitioners, fae, and the magically curious - e.g.
  **The Chestnut Tree (19)** at Marble Arch (on the old Tyburn gallows site;
  managed by part-fae **Wanda Pourier**; freehold now Lady Ty's), **The Goat and
  Crocodile** (Shoreditch), and **Spaniards Inn** (Highgate). Others sit on the
  fringe (the Rising Sun) or deep within (a no-name pub under Oxford Street).
- **Nazareths** (formerly "goblin fairs"): pop-up truce markets for the
  supernatural community, recorded since at least `1534`. Rules: **no weapons; no
  glamours/masks/helmets; *Pax Domus* (House Peace) - no fights**. A dubious shadow
  economy; police (called "the Isaacs") are unwelcome - if you must attend, send
  someone unknown.
