# Notable Items & Artefacts

<!-- Static world reference. Original, spoiler-free summary of widely-known setting facts; no plot, no quoted text. Player-safe at this general level. -->

The kinds of magical object a reader of the books would recognise, at
setting-knowledge level — what they are and why they matter, not where any
specific one is in a case. Game mechanics for several of these live in the
advanced rules (`../rules-advanced/09-magic.md`); this is the in-world picture.

## A wizard's staff

A practitioner's staff is, in plain terms, a rechargeable battery for magic — a
store of power the wielder can draw on in addition to their own. Making one is a
serious undertaking of magical metalwork and long labour, so staffs tend to be
inherited, awarded, or recovered rather than knocked up on demand. They are
prized, personal, and not lightly handed over. A staff need not look like a
staff: a senior practitioner might carry one disguised as an ordinary
silver-topped walking cane.

## The foundational text of English magic

English (Newtonian) magic rests on a foundational treatise attributed to Sir
Isaac Newton — the work that set out magic as a disciplined, learnable science of
*formae* rather than folk superstition. It is the root text an apprentice's whole
training descends from, and the reason the tradition bears Newton's name. Copies
and related works live in the Folly's library and archive; some magical books are
themselves dangerous and are kept secured.

## Demon traps

A demon trap is, bluntly, a magical anti-personnel mine — a nasty, feared device
rather than anything an investigator would casually make. They are recognised
more by their magical "feel" than their appearance, can be disguised in mundane
objects, and are dangerous to approach or disarm. Their existence is part of why
practitioners are wary of unfamiliar magical workings.

## Rose jars

A rose jar is a vessel that holds a trapped ghost or similar entity — an occupied
one gives off a soft glow. They turn up as unusual "witnesses," as dangerous
evidence, or as things that must be recovered before the wrong person misuses
them. Handling one carelessly destroys what's inside.

## Cold iron

Cold iron is the classic material that unsettles and can harm much of the fae and
the demi-monde. It is a practical consideration in any tense dealing with such
beings, and a reason some of them avoid certain places and objects. (See the
demi-monde material in `magic-overview.md` and the rules.)

## Enchanted objects in general

Beyond these, the world allows for enchanted items more broadly — objects worked
to hold power, carry a spell, or store magic. They are GM-facing and uncommon
rather than off-the-shelf gear; an enchanted object encountered in play is a
notable find, not standard equipment.
