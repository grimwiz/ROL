# Combat

<!-- Source: core-rules, rules-summaries. Status: reviewed-complete. -->

## Combat Frame

Combat uses structured rounds when the outcome of violence, restraint, escape, or another dangerous confrontation needs step-by-step resolution.

Combat should be fast. Most fights last only a few rounds, because `3` total damage takes a character out of the fight. Investigators have Luck, so defeat is more common than death.

Before play, agree the table's acceptable level of violence in description. The mechanics work the same whether narration is restrained or graphic.

## Starting Combat

When combat begins, ask each involved player what their investigator is doing. Convert the described action into the relevant skill only after the goal is clear.

If there is disagreement over who initiates the first action:

1. The character with the highest DEX goes first.
2. If DEX is tied, use the normal tiebreaker rules in `02-core-resolution.md`.

Melee combat is usually opposed, so acting first does not guarantee hitting first.

## Combat Rounds and Turn Order

A combat round is elastic time. During one round, each investigator and active NPC gets one turn to initiate one action.

After the first turn in the combat scene resolves:

1. The character who initiated combat rolls a die.
2. Even means play passes to their left.
3. Odd means play passes to their right.
4. Use that direction for the whole combat scene.

When play returns to the starting character or GM and everyone has acted, the round ends. If the conflict is unresolved, start another round in the same order.

For online play, the GM should set a fixed player order at the start of combat and keep it even if the video-call display changes.

## NPC Turns

The GM controls all NPCs.

If an NPC starts combat:

- Resolve that NPC's turn.
- Roll to set the direction of play.
- When play returns to the GM, resolve the remaining NPCs in any order.
- The next round starts with the NPC who initiated the first round.

If an investigator starts combat, the GM resolves NPC turns when the table order reaches the GM.

## Actions on a Turn

On a turn, a character may initiate one action, such as:

- Melee attack with Fighting.
- Firearms attack with Firearms.
- Spell attack with Magic, if the character has Magic.
- Flee, possibly requiring an opposed Fighting roll if already engaged.
- Another time-consuming action, possibly requiring a roll.
- Reload a firearm.

Simple speech does not use a turn. Moving more than a short distance may use a turn.

Drawing an easily accessible weapon is normally free. Drawing a weapon from an awkward location, such as a handgun in a briefcase, uses the turn; the weapon is ready next round.

## Movement in Combat

Distances are usually handled in theatre-of-the-mind terms. The GM makes the final call on positions, range, and whether a target is reachable.

Movement limits:

- A character can move up to their MOV in metres/yards and still act.
- A character can move up to five times MOV in metres/yards by using their whole turn.
- Arm's length means about 1 metre/yard.
- Point-blank range for firearms, spells, and ranged attacks is 3 metres/yards or less.

## Six-Step Combat Procedure

Use this procedure for melee, firearms, spell combat, vehicle combat, and other combat-like exchanges.

1. Set your goal: the acting character describes what they are trying to do.
2. Target responds: the target describes how they react.
3. Dice rolls: resolve opposed or unopposed rolls.
4. Damage roll: if the winning goal inflicts damage, determine damage.
5. Narration: describe the result, led by the winner where appropriate.
6. Next player's turn: pass play to the next character in the order.

## Step 1: Set Your Goal

The acting character describes the intended action in fictional terms. The goal matters because not every combat action is an attempt to cause damage.

Examples of goals:

- Hurt or incapacitate someone.
- Restrain, disarm, move, or knock someone prone.
- Shoot, throw, or cast at a target.
- Escape the fight.
- Complete another task while fighting continues.

## Step 2: Target Responds

The target chooses a response. Responses are reactions to being targeted; they are not the same as initiating an action on the target's own turn.

Available responses:

| Response | Use limit |
|---|---|
| Do nothing | Unlimited |
| Dodge or dive for cover | Unlimited |
| Fight back | Once per combat round, or twice with Fast Reactions |
| Flee | Unlimited if an escape route exists |

Only Fight Back has the once-per-round limit. A character may dodge, flee, or do nothing against multiple attacks in the same round.

## Response: Do Nothing

If the target does nothing:

| Attack type | Handling |
|---|---|
| Melee | No attack roll is needed; the attacker makes the damage roll. |
| Firearms | The attacker makes an unopposed Firearms roll. |
| Magic | The attacker makes an unopposed Magic roll. |

## Response: Dodge or Dive for Cover

Use two different procedures.

Melee dodge:

- The target rolls Fighting in the opposed roll.
- This also covers avoiding thrown items; the thrower uses Athletics.

Dive for cover against firearms or magic:

- The attacker makes the unopposed Firearms or Magic roll with one penalty die.
- The target must forgo their next action.
- If the target has not acted yet this round, they lose their turn this round.
- If the target has already acted, they lose their turn in the next round.

## Response: Fight Back

A character can fight back once per combat round. Fast Reactions permits a second fight-back response in the same round.

Fight back options:

| Method | Requirement | Roll |
|---|---|---|
| Melee | Attacker is within arm's length | Fighting opposed roll |
| Firearm | Loaded firearm already in hand | Firearms opposed roll |
| Magic | Character can cast a spell | Magic opposed roll |

If the defender wins the opposed roll, their counterattack hits or succeeds and the original attack is negated.

Melee fight back can be used against any attack from within arm's length, including a firearm or spell. It may also be used for a combat manoeuvre.

If a non-damaging spell is used to disrupt an attacker, the target may need a POW roll to maintain concentration. On a failed POW roll, the attack fails.

## Response: Flee

The target can flee only if there is an escape route.

Against melee:

- Resolve an opposed Fighting roll.
- If the fleeing character wins, they avoid the attack and get away.
- If the attacker wins, the fleeing character suffers the attack before fleeing, if still able.

Against firearms or magic:

- The fleeing character moves at full speed, up to five times MOV in metres/yards.
- The attacker makes an unopposed Firearms or Magic roll.
- If the fleeing character's MOV is 8 or higher, the attacker has one penalty die for a fast-moving target.
- If MOV is 7 or lower, this penalty does not apply, though other cover or range changes may matter.

A character who flees must forgo their next action, using the same timing as diving for cover.

## Step 3: Dice Rolls

Most melee exchanges use opposed combat rolls. Ranged attacks are usually unopposed unless the target fights back.

Combat skills:

| Action | Skill |
|---|---|
| Hand-to-hand or melee weapon | Fighting |
| Thrown weapon or object | Athletics |
| Firearm or bow | Firearms |
| Spell | Magic |

Opposed combat uses the normal success levels:

```text
Critical > Hard > Regular > Weak success > Fumble
```

If success levels tie in combat, both sides may spend Luck to improve their own result. If the tie remains, the character whose turn it is wins. Combat ties do not use highest skill as the final tiebreaker.

Combat rolls cannot be pushed.

## Unopposed Ranged Attacks

Use unopposed ranged attack rolls when the target does nothing, dives for cover, or flees rather than fighting back.

Set difficulty by range:

| Range | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Within standard range | Regular |
| Beyond standard range, up to maximum range | Hard |

All ranged spells use the same range as a regular handgun unless the spell's own text changes the effect.

## Firearms Reference

To determine hit difficulty, use the range bands below. To determine damage, add the base weapon damage to the damage roll result in `07-damage-and-healing.md`.

| Firearm | Base damage | Standard range | Maximum range |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Bow | 1 | 30 metres/yards | 100 metres/yards |
| Crossbow | 1 | 50 metres/yards | 150 metres/yards |
| Heavy handgun | 2 | 15 metres/yards | 50 metres/yards |
| Machine gun | 2 | 100 metres/yards | 300 metres/yards |
| Regular handgun | 1 | 15 metres/yards | 50 metres/yards |
| Rifle | 2 | 100 metres/yards | 300 metres/yards |
| Shotgun | 2 | 50 metres/yards | 150 metres/yards |
| Submachine gun | 2 | 15 metres/yards | 50 metres/yards |

A rifle's maximum effective range can be doubled to 600 metres/yards if it is effectively braced and fitted with a telescopic sight.

## Step 4: Damage Roll

If the winning goal inflicts harm, make a damage roll using `07-damage-and-healing.md`.

If the goal was a combat manoeuvre, spell effect, escape, restraint, or other non-damage outcome, apply that outcome instead. If the outcome obviously causes injury, use the other-damage rules in `07-damage-and-healing.md`.

## Step 5: Narration

Narration should follow the declared goals and dice result. The winner normally leads the description, with the GM ensuring it fits the scene.

Use the surroundings when relevant. A fight in an office, sewer, pub, or street should produce different consequences and opportunities.

## Step 6: Next Player's Turn

When the turn ends, pass play to the next character in the established order. If everyone has acted and the conflict is unresolved, begin the next round.

## Combat Manoeuvres

Use a combat manoeuvre when the goal is not primarily damage: restrain, disarm, move, knock down, handcuff, or similar.

Rules:

- Roll Fighting in the opposed combat roll.
- If both sides use manoeuvres, both roll Fighting.
- If the target's STR exceeds the manoeuvring character's STR by 50 or more, the manoeuvre automatically fails.

On a winning manoeuvre, choose an outcome that fits the goal:

| Outcome | Effect |
|---|---|
| Restrain | Target has one penalty die on actions until released or they escape. |
| Knock prone | Later Fighting attacks against the prone target gain one bonus die. The prone character may stand on their turn as a free action. |
| Handcuff | Can be applied to an already restrained character. |
| Disarm | The held item is knocked down. If the manoeuvre wins by two or more success levels, the manoeuvring character may take the item. |
| Move | Move the target up to 5 metres/yards. |

A restrained character, or another character helping them, can attack the restrainer. A successful attack releases the restrained character.

## De-Escalation and Police Training

Police characters are trained to de-escalate before using force. If an officer tries to talk someone down, resolve it as an opposed Social skill roll. The opposition may be Read Person, POW, or another appropriate value.

If the officer is in uniform or shows a warrant card, apply the relevant police occupation bonus die where that advantage applies.

## Improvised Weapons

Improvised weapons use:

- Fighting in melee.
- Athletics when thrown.

If an improvised weapon is awkward or unwieldy, the GM may impose a penalty die.

Improvised weapons have base weapon damage `1`.

If it is uncertain whether a useful improvised weapon is present, call for a Luck roll.

## Outnumbered

Outnumbered applies only to melee attacks.

If a character attacks a target who has already been targeted by someone else this combat round, the attacker gets one bonus die on their Fighting roll.

The target still gets to respond to each attack. The outnumbered bonus represents the attacker's advantage after the target has already had to defend themselves this round.

Fast Reactions changes this:

- A character with Fast Reactions can fight back twice per round.
- A character with Fast Reactions is never outnumbered, so attackers do not gain the outnumbered bonus against them.

## Armour

| Protection | Effect |
|---|---|
| Natural Toughness | Reduce unarmed and blunt melee weapon damage by 1. Does not reduce knives, bullets, or damage-causing spells. |
| Regular armour | Reduce melee damage by 1. Does not reduce firearms or certain spell attacks. |
| Ballistic armour | Reduce melee and firearms damage by 2. Does not reduce certain spell attacks. |

Natural Toughness stacks with worn armour when both apply.

Armour does not protect against poison, drowning, and some magical effects. Use individual spell rules where they differ.

## Ranged Attack Modifiers

Add all relevant bonus and penalty dice, cancel them one-for-one, then apply the combat excess rule below.

| Modifier | Effect |
|---|---|
| Cover or concealment hides at least half the target | One penalty die to Firearms or Magic. |
| Point-blank range, 3 metres/yards or less | One bonus die to Firearms, Magic, or another ranged attack. |
| Aiming | Spend a turn aiming; next turn gain one bonus die if not damaged and not moved. |
| Fast-moving target, MOV 8 or higher at full speed | One penalty die to Firearms or Magic. |
| Very small target, roughly small dog size or smaller | One penalty die. |
| Large target, roughly standard car size or larger | One bonus die. |
| Multiple shots | Choose either one bonus die to hit or increased damage. |

## Cover Protection

If an attack passes through cover, subtract the cover's protection from damage before applying it to the target.

| Cover material | Blocks |
|---|---:|
| 2.5 cm / 1 inch plywood | 1 damage |
| 2.5 cm / 1 inch brick or concrete | 2 damage |
| 4 cm / 1.5 inch bulletproof glass | 2 damage |
| 5 mm / 3/16 inch steel plate | 2 damage |
| Large sandbag | 3 damage |

## Aiming

To aim:

1. Declare aiming on the character's turn.
2. Use the whole turn aiming.
3. Attack on that character's turn in the next round.
4. Gain one bonus die if the character did nothing else, did not move, and did not suffer damage before the attack.

Only one bonus die can be gained from aiming.

There are no separate called-shot mechanics. If a player aims at a specific body part, interpret the attack and damage result narratively.

## Reloading

Reloading a firearm normally uses one combat round as the character's action.

A character may reload on their turn and later in the same round fire that weapon as a Fight Back response if they are attacked and the situation permits it.

## Multiple Shots

Multiple shots means three rapid shots at a single target. It applies to suitable firearms and to certain spells only where the spell supports it.

Make one attack roll. Before rolling, choose one option:

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| Increased chance to hit | Gain one bonus die to hit; roll damage normally once. |
| Increased damage | Do not gain the multiple-shots bonus die; if the attack hits, double the damage indicated by the damage roll's success level, then add base weapon damage. |

The increased-damage choice must be made before the attack roll. If the attack fails, the attacker cannot retroactively choose the bonus die.

## Shooting Into Melee

If a firearms or spell attack into melee fumbles, the GM may decide the attack hits a random ally instead.

If an investigator fumbles, the GM may use present investigators' Luck values to determine who is hit where that is appropriate.

## Ranged and Thrown Weapons

Ranged missile weapons, such as bows and crossbows, use the firearms attack procedure.

Thrown weapons:

- Use Athletics.
- Resolve like melee Fighting attacks.
- If thrown beyond standard range, apply one penalty die.

## Multiple Bonus or Penalty Dice in Combat

Unlike ordinary rolls, combat has an overflow rule after cancellation.

| Net dice after cancellation | Attack roll | If the attack succeeds |
|---|---|---|
| Two or more bonus dice | Roll with one bonus die | Damage roll gains one bonus die. |
| Two or more penalty dice | Roll with one penalty die | Damage roll takes one penalty die. |

Only one bonus or penalty die is applied to the attack roll itself.

## Critical Successes and Fumbles

A critical success in combat works normally and also gives the attacker one bonus die on the damage roll.

A fumble gives the GM permission to add a serious complication. Possibilities include dropped or broken weapons, self-injury for 1 damage, an ally hit by a missed ranged attack, new enemies arriving, or a firearm malfunction.

## Firearms Malfunctions

On a fumbled Firearms attack, the GM may decide the weapon fails to fire.

| Weapon type | Likely malfunction |
|---|---|
| Revolver, break-open gun, bolt-action rifle | Dud round |
| Lever-action or automatic firearm | Jam |

Clearing a jam in combat requires a successful Mechanical Repair or Firearms roll. The user may try once per combat round on their turn until successful.

Outside combat, clearing a jam may be easy enough to require no roll.
