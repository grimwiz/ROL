# Chases

<!-- Source: core-rules, rules-summaries. Status: reviewed-complete. -->

## Chase Frame

Use chase rules when pursuit matters and the scene should be more than one roll.

Chases use rounds, like combat, but they track momentum through abstract locations rather than exact distance and speed. Each location should present one main challenge: open sprinting, climbing, crossing traffic, pushing through crowds, navigating a Tube station, and so on.

A chase assumes the fleeing character has an escape route. If they are trapped, they must first create a way out. If they are already in combat, they may need to use the Flee response in `06-combat.md` before the chase begins.

## Starting a Chase

If there is disagreement over who acts first:

1. Highest DEX goes first.
2. If tied, use normal tiebreakers.

After the first turn resolves:

1. The first character rolls a die.
2. Even means play passes left.
3. Odd means play passes right.
4. Continue in that direction for the whole chase.

If the chase starts from an existing combat, keep the existing combat order.

In a chase, the GM has one turn for all NPCs. NPCs do not make chase rolls for movement; they move automatically. The GM rolls only when an NPC attacks or does something else that requires a roll.

## Location Questions and Luck

The GM describes the current location. A player may ask whether a useful element exists: an open back door, a train about to leave, an unlocked gate, a crowd, roadworks, or similar.

When chance is the real question, call for Luck.

If investigators are fleeing:

- Success means the location favours the investigator.
- Failure means the location favours the pursuer.

If investigators are pursuing:

- Use a group Luck roll if several investigators are involved.
- Success means the location works against the fleeing NPC.
- Failure means the location favours the fleeing NPC.

## Chase Turn Structure

For an investigator who is fleeing or pursuing:

1. Establish the location and intended route.
2. Make the chase roll if required.
3. Track chase successes and chase failures.
4. Make a ranged attack if desired and permitted, or pass to the next turn.

## Chase Roll: Burst of Speed

Use Burst of Speed when the location is mostly open and speed is the main factor.

Compare the investigator's MOV with the relevant NPC's MOV.

| MOV comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| Investigator MOV is higher | Automatic chase success; no roll. |
| Investigator MOV is equal | Roll DEX. |
| Investigator MOV is lower | Automatic chase failure; no roll. |

If a DEX roll is required, set difficulty from the NPC's DEX.

| NPC DEX | Difficulty |
|---:|---|
| 69 or lower | Regular |
| 70 or higher | Hard |

On success, gain one chase success. On failure, incur one chase failure.

## Chase Roll: Negotiating Obstacles

Use Negotiating Obstacles when the location has a hazard or barrier: wall, fence, stream, gap, traffic, crowd, difficult stairs, debris, or similar.

Roll Athletics.

Set difficulty from the NPC's Athletics.

| NPC Athletics | Difficulty |
|---:|---|
| 69 or lower | Regular |
| 70 or higher | Hard |

On success, gain one chase success. On failure, incur one chase failure.

## Spending Luck and Pushing Chase Rolls

If an investigator fails a DEX or Athletics chase roll, the player may:

- Accept one chase failure.
- Spend Luck to make the roll succeed.
- Push the roll if the fiction allows a changed approach or extra risk.

Failure on a pushed chase roll should carry a serious consequence. The default consequence is losing the chase: the fleeing investigator is caught, or the pursuing investigator loses the quarry.

Automatic chase failures from lower MOV are not failed rolls, so they cannot be pushed. The GM may still allow other actions or Luck-based environmental questions where appropriate.

## Tracking Successes and Failures

Each investigator tracks their own chase successes and failures.

The basic race is first to two:

- Two chase successes before two chase failures means the investigator wins their objective.
- Two chase failures before two chase successes means the investigator loses their objective.

After a chase reaches the catch-up state, use the follow-on rules below rather than resetting the entire chase.

## Investigators Fleeing

The investigator is trying to get away from one or more NPCs.

If the investigator gains two chase successes before two failures:

- They outpace the pursuer.
- The chase ends.
- They get away.

If the investigator suffers two chase failures before two successes:

- The pursuer catches up.
- On the GM's next turn, the pursuer may attack, grab, trip, restrain, or otherwise interfere.

If the pursuer fails to incapacitate, restrain, or stop the investigator, the chase can continue.

From that point:

- Each investigator chase failure means the NPC stays in range and can attack again on the GM's turn.
- Two consecutive investigator chase successes mean the investigator outpaces the pursuer and escapes.
- Each investigator chase success moves them ahead and out of melee range until the pursuer catches up again.

## GM Turn While Pursuing

On the GM's turn:

1. Move Forward: describe the pursuing NPCs moving through the location. Do not roll for their movement.
2. Combat or Next Turn: if an NPC has caught an investigator, resolve an attack or manoeuvre if desired.

When an NPC attacks a fleeing investigator during a chase:

- Use the combat procedure in `06-combat.md`.
- The fleeing investigator cannot fight back without stopping.
- Because both are moving at speed, treat the fleeing target as dodging.
- If the attack makes the investigator Down, restrained, or prone, the chase ends or shifts fully into combat.
- If not, the investigator may continue fleeing on their next turn or stop and engage.

## Investigators Pursuing

The investigators are trying to catch one or more NPCs.

If an investigator suffers two chase failures before two successes:

- They lose the chase.
- The NPC escapes out of sight.

If an investigator gains two chase successes before two failures:

- They catch up with the NPC.
- They may try to attack, grab, trip, restrain, or otherwise stop the NPC.

If the investigator does not incapacitate, restrain, or stop the NPC, the chase can continue.

From that point:

- Each investigator chase failure means the NPC moves out of range.
- Two consecutive investigator chase failures mean the NPC escapes.
- Each investigator chase success means the investigator catches up and can attack or manoeuvre on their next turn.

## GM Turn While Fleeing

On the GM's turn:

1. Move Forward: describe the fleeing NPCs moving through the location. Do not roll for their movement.
2. Combat or Next Turn: fleeing NPCs may make ranged attacks under the mid-chase ranged attack rules.

When an investigator catches and attacks a fleeing NPC:

- Use the combat procedure in `06-combat.md`.
- The fleeing NPC cannot fight back without stopping.
- Because both are moving at speed, treat the fleeing target as dodging.
- If the NPC is Down, restrained, or prone, the chase ends or shifts fully into combat.
- If not, the NPC may continue fleeing on its next turn or stop and engage.

## Multiple NPCs

If investigators are being chased by a group of NPCs:

- Focus chase mechanics on the fastest NPC.
- If the fastest NPC catches an investigator, use GM judgement or a Luck roll to decide whether the rest soon catch up.

If investigators are chasing a group of NPCs:

- If the investigators choose one target, focus on that NPC and let the others escape.
- If catching any one NPC is enough, focus on the slowest NPC and let the others escape.

If investigators split up to chase different NPCs, run multiple chases. The GM may resolve them one at a time or cut between them.

## Ranged Attacks During Chases

After resolving any chase roll on their turn, a character may make a Firearms or Magic attack.

| Situation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Character keeps moving and attacks | Attack roll has one penalty die. |
| Investigator stops moving to attack | No movement penalty, but their chase progress this round is an automatic chase failure. |
| Lone NPC stops moving to attack | No movement penalty; after the GM's turn, all pursuing investigators gain one additional chase success. |
| Some NPCs stop and others continue | Use the multiple-NPC guidance. |

Other ranged modifiers from `06-combat.md` can also apply.

## Vehicle Chases

Vehicle chases use the same structure as foot chases.

Changes:

- For a vehicle Burst of Speed, use Drive instead of DEX.
- For a vehicle Negotiating Obstacles, use Drive instead of Athletics.
- Use vehicle MOV and Build where the vehicle table applies.

## Vehicular Combat

Use vehicular combat when drivers use vehicles against each other, usually to ram, force off the road, box in, or disable another vehicle.

Use the normal six combat steps from `06-combat.md`, with these changes:

- Drivers use Drive for the opposed combat roll.
- The default damaging outcome is a collision.
- Do not make a separate damage roll for vehicles; calculate damage from relative Build.

Vehicle collision damage:

| Build comparison | Damage |
|---|---|
| One vehicle's Build is more than double the other's | Higher-Build vehicle takes 0; lower-Build vehicle takes 3. |
| One vehicle's Build is higher, up to double the other's | Higher-Build vehicle takes 1; lower-Build vehicle takes 2. |
| Builds are equal | Both vehicles take 2. |

## Vehicle Damage Conditions

Vehicle damage is cumulative.

| Total vehicle damage | Condition | Effect |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Pranged | Still drivable. |
| 2 | Damaged | Still drivable, barely. |
| 3 | Wrecked | No longer drivable; out of the chase. |

When a vehicle is wrecked, narrate the outcome according to speed and surroundings. Driver and passenger injuries may follow from the control roll rules.

## Control Rolls

Whenever a vehicle is damaged in a collision or the driver suffers damage, the driver must make a Drive roll to maintain control.

Modifiers:

- If the vehicle suffered 2 damage in one collision, apply one penalty die.
- Bicycles and motorcycles involved in a collision always apply one penalty die to the Drive roll.
- One or more burst tyres impose one penalty die on Drive rolls.

If the control roll fails, the vehicle crashes. The vehicle, driver, and passengers suffer damage based on speed.

| Speed | Damage to vehicle and occupants |
|---|---:|
| Low speed, under about 40 kph / 25 mph | 2 |
| Medium speed, up to about 120 kph / 75 mph | 3 |
| High speed, over about 120 kph / 75 mph | 4 |

## Firearms Against Vehicles

Vehicle damage is on a different scale from character damage. Firearms usually disable a vehicle by targeting the driver or tyres rather than by reducing vehicle damage directly.

Tyres:

- Count as small targets, so apply one penalty die.
- A high-speed moving vehicle may impose another penalty die.
- Roll damage normally.
- `1` damage punctures a tyre.
- A vehicle with one or more burst tyres takes one penalty die on Drive rolls.

Drivers:

- A visible driver usually counts as a small target because only part of them can be seen through the vehicle.
- Resolve damage against the driver normally.
- If the driver is damaged, make a control roll.

## Repairs

After vehicular combat, repairs are handled narratively. The GM decides the time and cost.

If it is uncertain whether a vehicle can be repaired at all, call for a Luck roll.

- Success means it can be fixed and returned to use.
- Failure means it is a write-off.

Luck rolls cannot be pushed or modified by spending Luck.
