GM- and table-facing framing for running Rivers of London: what the game is at
the table, how to set up a session, what each person may read (spoiler control),
and the tone of the setting. The mechanical frame — dice, glossary, and roll
logic — is in `../00-system-overview.md` and is not repeated here.

## What The Game Is At The Table

- The goal is to have fun telling a dramatic, collaborative story; there are no
  winners or losers as in a board game. The players work together toward a common
  goal while the group tells the most interesting story it can.
- One person is the **Game Moderator (GM)**, who runs the game for everyone else.
  The others play **investigators** — newly recruited members of the Folly — who
  figure out the mysteries the GM presents.
- A useful image: the GM is like a film director and a **case file is like an
  unfinished script** — a framework to build on, with the details improvised
  together at the table. The GM sets the scene and plays the NPCs; the players say
  how their investigators react; dice settle the uncertain, dramatic moments.
- Roll dice when there is a dramatic effect or a twist either way — does the
  investigator catch the suspect, or do they get away? The rules and the dice let
  the GM decide such moments fairly and without bias.

## Setting Up A Session

What a group needs to play:

- This rulebook (its content, as surfaced by the app).
- Roleplaying dice or a dice-roller (see `../00-system-overview.md` → Dice).
- Paper, pencils, an eraser, and character sheets.
- In person: somewhere quiet, with a table big enough to roll dice.
- Online: a video-conferencing platform everyone can use.
- Time: about **two to four hours** per session — roughly two hours online, up to
  three or four in person.

Onboarding path the book recommends:

- New players can start with the solo adventure **The Domestic** (out of scope for
  this corpus — it is solo, non-GM material) to learn key concepts through play.
- A group can then run the built-in introductory case (Chapter 8), surfaced in the
  app as the **Bookshop** sandbox case (`../../canonical/cases/bookshop/`), which
  walks a new GM through their first case file step by step.
- The rules are deliberately light; nobody needs to memorise them before playing,
  and rules summaries (cheat sheets) back up play.

## Who Reads What (Spoiler Control)

This is a GM-facing concern and underpins the app's player_safe gating:

- **Players may read:** the rules and setting orientation — broadly Chapters 1–5,
  and parts of Chapter 7 for setting colour. This maps to the player-facing rules
  corpus plus the player-safe scenario gazetteer.
- **GM only:** Chapters 6 and 8 contain secrets and surprises that are spoiled by
  reading them — Chapter 6 (the Rogues' Gallery NPC/being profiles) and the
  Chapter 8 case in particular. Keep these GM-only.
- The printed book flags spoilers with **footnotes naming the source novel**; the
  paraphrased corpus trims those novel citations but preserves the same GM-only vs
  player-safe split. See `npcs-and-beings.md` (GM-facing index) and the
  per-persona `player_safe` framing.

## What Sort Of Investigators People Play

- Investigators are newly recruited members of the Folly. Each can be a **police
  officer** or a **civilian consultant** — the player's choice, agreed with the GM
  so the concept fits the group's intended stories.
- Police investigators bring law-enforcement knowledge and powers (Warrant Card);
  civilian consultants bring their own expertise (history, languages, science) and
  carry a **Folly Consultant's Card** that gets them into places they otherwise
  could not go, though with fewer powers than a warranted officer.
- Any investigator may also be a **Newtonian apprentice** (able to cast spells and
  detect magic) or a less formal **hedge wizard** — or have no magic at all and
  contribute purely through mundane expertise. A mix of backgrounds makes for a
  better game. See `../03-character-creation.md`.

## Setting Tone

- The default era is **London, 2016**, shortly after the events of *False Value*.
  Magic is real but not public knowledge; London's rivers are home to powerful
  genii locorum. The in-world reason a fresh team of investigators exists is the
  sanctioned **Folly Expansion** — the unit recruiting officers and consultants.
- The world is **firmly rooted in the British class system** — working, middle,
  and upper class — and class shapes how characters are treated, on top of gender
  and ethnicity. Movement between classes is possible but not always easy or
  welcome; "Old Boy Networks" from elite schools still advantage their members.
  This is tone and texture for portrayal, not a mechanic.
