# Demi-Monde Rules

<!-- Source: rogues-and-demi-monde. Status: reviewed-complete. -->

This file covers mechanical treatment for fae, genii locorum, ghosts, vampires, Quiet People, and other demi-monde beings. NPC biographies, recurring-character summaries, London context, case-use notes, and optional demi-monde investigator rules belong outside the basic rules corpus.

## Scope and Terms

The terms demi-monde and fae are imprecise in-world labels. For rules purposes, demi-monde includes people and beings touched or changed by magic.

Common routes into the demi-monde:

- Born different or born partly fae.
- Changed by magic, Fairyland, or deliberate magical alteration.
- Human practitioners who seek out and learn magic.

Fae are not necessarily dangerous, but they are not fully human. High Fae live outside the normal material world in Fairyland. Lower fae and other demi-monde beings can exist in ordinary society.

## Demi-Monde Profile Fields

Demi-monde profiles use the same basic characteristic, skill, language, advantage, and damage fields as human NPCs, with these additions:

- `Powers`: listed powers such as The Glamour or Luck of the Demi-Monde.
- `Demi-monde affinity`: the sphere of thematic power, if any.
- `Vestigia`: the sensory impression detected with Sense Vestigia, if specific enough to matter.
- `Other` or `Special`: creature-specific exceptions.

Higher demi-monde beings, especially genii locorum, usually have distinctive vestigia. Extremely powerful beings may not require a roll to sense. Lower demi-monde beings often register only as something other unless their thematic power leaves a specific trace.

## The Glamour

Genii locorum and many fae can sway human minds with a word, look, or similar exercise of power. The target rolls POW. Use the target's awareness to choose the result table.

### Uninformed Target

Use this when the target does not know the glamour exists or has not been taught how to resist it.

| POW result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Fumble or failure | Target obeys without question and never thinks to question why. |
| Regular or Hard success | Target obeys, but later wonders why and rationalises it. |
| Critical success | Target is unaffected, reacts as if the request were mundane, and is unaware of magic. |

### Informed Target

Use this when the target has been told about the glamour and how to resist it.

| POW result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Fumble | Target behaves exactly as bidden, as if uninformed. |
| Failure | Target begins to act, then recovers self-control. They are aware of the glamour. |
| Regular, Hard, or Critical success | Target is aware of the glamour and is not compelled. |

If an investigator is targeted, they may spend Luck to overcome a failed POW roll. They may also push the POW roll as an attempt to resist; a second failure places them fully at the glamour user's mercy.

## Luck of the Demi-Monde

Non-human demi-monde characters have Luck points.

Rules:

- Luck equals POW.
- Luck refreshes fully every day at a fixed time.
- Demi-monde characters spend Luck under the normal Luck rules unless a specific power gives a different use.
- Their Luck can support acts that seem improbably skilful or luck-defying.

For NPCs, set the fixed refresh time only if it matters in play.

## Gifted Luck

Some powerful demi-monde beings can grant a Luck pool to another character as a blessing, boon, or pact.

Rules:

- The gifted Luck pool is separate from the recipient's normal Luck.
- The GM sets the size of the pool; 50 points is a source example for a major river-spirit boon.
- The recipient may spend the pool while acting within the pact's purpose.
- Spending against the pact, abusing the gift, or breaking the agreement can create retaliation, debt, or recompense.

## Thematic Power

Genii locorum, fae, and some other demi-monde beings have a sphere of influence called an affinity. Examples include water, earth, locks, plants, animals, air, fire, or machinery.

For NPC demi-monde powers:

| Manifestation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Minor | A colour or character moment that does not solve a case, advance an agenda, or provide a meaningful mechanical benefit. Any demi-monde NPC can perform minor manifestations freely, though overuse weakens impact. |
| Major | A meaningful use that furthers a plot, resolves a case problem, or provides an in-game benefit. A demi-monde NPC can usually perform one major manifestation per case file. |

Major manifestations are demanding and may create emotional, energetic, social, or political costs.

Genii locorum are not balanced against investigators. Their thematic powers should normally remain story-based rather than costed.

## Codes of Conduct

Demi-monde beings are constrained by obligations, agreements, territory, etiquette, and the consequences of accepting hospitality.

Mechanical use:

- Codes of conduct justify why powerful demi-monde beings do not use full power constantly.
- Breaking agreements or using power without permission can create supernatural, social, or political consequences.
- Food and drink from demi-monde hosts may carry obligation unless explicitly offered free of it.
- Permission from an appropriate authority can matter when a being is bound by an agreement, such as a peace or territorial compact.

## Cold Iron

Some demi-monde beings are vulnerable to cold iron.

| Vulnerable | Not vulnerable by default |
|---|---|
| High Fae, including Molly and Foxglove; unicorns; genii locorum. | Chimeras, talking foxes, vampires, lower fae, trolls, Quiet People, and beings like Zachary Palmer. |

Rules for vulnerable targets:

- Presence of large amounts of cold iron or direct contact may impose a penalty die on the vulnerable character's actions.
- A successful attack with a cold iron weapon gains a bonus die on the damage roll.
- A vulnerable character wounded by cold iron cannot recover until the iron is removed.
- Until removal, the character remains on the damage condition they had at the end of combat.
- Cold iron wounds cannot be treated by human medicine.
- If the iron remains in the body, it drains the victim's life force until death.
- Treat cold iron recovery narratively through High Fae, genii locorum, or similarly appropriate supernatural intervention.

## Genii Locorum

A genius loci is a spirit of place. Genii locorum are usually tied to a location, river, canal, building, or similar domain.

Rules and table use:

- They use demi-monde powers, not Newtonian magic.
- Their thematic power is story-scale rather than player-scale.
- They may have the glamour.
- They have demi-monde Luck.
- Many are vulnerable to cold iron.
- They can be killed, and destruction of their place can kill or weaken them.
- They often have distinctive vestigia; very powerful examples may be obvious without a roll.
- They may choose any Affluence level that fits the story.
- Gifts, ceremonies, territory, and etiquette matter when seeking favour.

Rules for player character genii locorum are not provided.

## Ghosts

Ghosts are imprints or recordings of personality left in an environment. They do not have normal statistics.

Rules:

- A ghost can usually be spoken with if it is present and responsive.
- If something has full statistics and claims to be a ghost, it is probably not a simple ghost.
- Werelight can attract ghosts and let them feed, which may make them visible and communicative.
- Revenants, poltergeist-like events, possessed objects, and non-corporeal genii locorum are separate case phenomena, not simple ghosts.

## Vampires

Vampires are former living creatures that feed on magical potential. They emit intense cold and are difficult to destroy.

Combat rules:

- Every time a vampire successfully inflicts damage in combat, the target also loses `1D6` magic points.
- If the vampire inflicts a mortal wound or fatal blow, the target loses all remaining magic points in addition to the wound's normal effects.
- Fireball has base weapon damage 2 against vampires. Boosted damage still increases by +1 per additional magic point.
- Flamethrowers inflict Deadly damage against vampires instead of Severe damage.
- Phosphorous grenades inflict Splat damage against vampires instead of Terminal damage.
- All other weapons inflict zero damage against vampires. Do not roll damage.
- Only fire completely destroys a vampire.
- Destroying a vampire by other means risks infection, contamination, or surviving pieces.

Infection:

- Anyone killed by a vampire risks becoming one.
- At death, the victim rolls CON.
- On a failure, the victim becomes a vampire unless the body is carefully burned.

## Unicorns

Unicorns are large, powerful, High Fae-adjacent beings.

Rules:

- They are normally invisible to human sight in the material world.
- They become visible in the presence of magic, such as a werelight.
- They are visible in Fairyland.
- They have Luck of the Demi-Monde.
- They are vulnerable to cold iron.
- A rider must succeed on Ride to convince a unicorn to accept being mounted.
- Unless the rider is used to handling unicorns, Ride is also required to stay in control when an ordinary horse would not require a roll.

## Quiet People

Quiet People are lower demi-monde beings associated with earth, clay, underground places, darkness, and quiet.

Common rules:

- Luck of the Demi-Monde.
- Earth affinity.
- Sense Vestigia 60 in sample profiles.
- Not vulnerable to cold iron by default.
- Often emit vestigia when using thematic powers: a momentary blast of heat and the smell of a pigsty are the profile examples.
- Bonus die to Observation in quiet and/or dimly lit locations.
- Penalty die to Observation in bright and/or noisy locations.

Quiet People cannot see in total darkness.

## Other Demi-Monde Types

Use these as mechanical flags rather than fixed limits.

| Type | Rules-facing notes |
|---|---|
| Chimeras | Magically altered human/animal hybrids. They can have Luck of the Demi-Monde and may use normal human language. |
| High Fae | Flesh-and-blood beings from Fairyland. They commonly have Luck of the Demi-Monde, nature-linked affinities, and cold iron vulnerability. Royal or powerful examples may have the glamour. |
| Lower fae | Broad category for beings such as Zachary Palmer. They can have demi-monde Luck and a narrow affinity, such as locks. They are not normally cold-iron vulnerable. |
| Talking foxes | Intelligent, speaking foxes. Use their profile skills and language notes; do not assume glamour or thematic power unless a case file gives it. |
| Trolls | Lower fae associated with rivers, banks, or bridges. Use demi-monde Luck and profile traits; not cold-iron vulnerable by default. |

## Non-Newtonian Special Abilities

Non-Newtonian practitioners and magical traditions do not need a full alternate spell system.

Options:

- Reskin a Chapter 4 spell with different gestures, terms, forms, or sensory presentation while keeping its order, magic point cost, Magic roll, and effect.
- Build a custom special ability that costs magic points and grants a narrow mechanical effect.
- Tie a tradition to a modified advantage, item, or discipline rather than to the normal Newtonian spell list.

### Mystic Art of Fighting

A trained practitioner may spend 1 magic point to gain a bonus die on a Fighting roll. This does not add a bonus die to the damage roll. Standard multiple bonus/penalty dice rules apply.

### Wizard's Sword

A wizard's sword is an enchanted magic point storage device. In the hands of a non-practitioner, it is a normal sword. In the hands of someone trained in the Mystic Art of Fighting, it is more effective.

Rules:

- Base weapon damage: 1.
- The sword can store magic points; NPC examples use 10 points for an apprentice and 20 for a master.
- A trained Mystic Art practitioner may spend 1 magic point to gain a bonus die on a damage roll made with the sword.
