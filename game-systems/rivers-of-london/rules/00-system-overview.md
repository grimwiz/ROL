## Game Frame

Rivers of London is a modern supernatural investigation RPG set around London and the Folly, the Metropolitan Police Service unit formally known as the Special Assessment Unit.

- One participant is the Game Moderator (GM): prepares and runs case files, portrays non-player characters, calls for rolls, and adjudicates consequences.
- Other participants play investigators: newly recruited Folly police officers, civilian consultants, magical practitioners, or other approved specialists.
- A case file is a scenario or adventure: the problem under investigation and the scenes, clues, NPCs, places, and conflicts that surround it.
- The default campaign frame is a team of investigators working together on strange crimes, dangerous magic, and demi-monde incidents.

## Core Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Character sheet | Record of an investigator's characteristics, skills, resources, background, and case notes. |
| Common skill | Broad skill available to all investigators, usually starting at 30%. |
| Combat skill | Fighting or Firearms; treated separately from common and expert skills. |
| Demi-monde | People, spirits, creatures, and communities altered by or connected to magic. |
| Expert skill | Specialist knowledge or training; usually starts at 00% unless selected. |
| Folly | The Met's supernatural investigation unit; also used for its headquarters and institutional culture. |
| Genius loci | Spirit of a place. Plural: genii locorum. |
| Hedge wizard | Folly term for a practitioner outside formal Newtonian training. |
| Investigator | A player-controlled character. |
| Newtonian magic | Spellcasting tradition derived from Isaac Newton's magical principles. |
| Practitioner | A person able to use magic. |
| Profile | Mechanical stat block for a character, NPC, or being. |
| Signare | A practitioner's distinctive magical signature. |
| Vestigia | Traces or impressions left by magic. |

## Dice

Required dice:

- Percentile dice (`D100`): two ten-sided dice read together as a number from 1 to 100.
- One six-sided die (`D6`).

Useful extra die:

- A third ten-sided die of a different colour for bonus and penalty dice.

Reading `D100`:

| Tens die | Units die | Result |
|---:|---:|---:|
| `00` | `0` | 100 |
| `00` | `1`-`9` | 1-9 |
| `10`-`90` | `0`-`9` | Tens plus units, e.g. `40` and `7` = 47 |

If using two single-digit dice, choose one colour as tens and one as units before rolling. `0` and `0` is 100.

## Basic Roll Logic

Most uncertain actions use percentile rolls. Lower is better.

1. State the investigator's goal.
2. The GM decides whether a roll is needed.
3. The GM selects the relevant skill or characteristic.
4. The GM sets the difficulty or calls for an opposed roll.
5. Roll `D100` and compare the result to the relevant value.
6. Apply success level, Luck, pushing, damage, or other subsystem rules as applicable.

Roll only when the outcome matters and failure changes the situation. Routine actions without dramatic pressure should usually happen without a roll.

## Success Levels

| Result | Basic meaning |
|---|---|
| Critical success | A roll of `01`. Best normal outcome category. |
| Hard success | Roll equal to or below half the skill or characteristic. |
| Regular success | Roll equal to or below the full skill or characteristic. |
| Failure | Roll above the full skill or characteristic. In opposed rolls this may be a weak success. |
| Fumble | A roll of `100`, unless another rule expands the fumble range. |

Full rules for difficulty, opposed rolls, pushing, Luck, and bonus/penalty dice belong in `02-core-resolution.md`.

## Default Player Knowledge

Player-facing rules can assume the following:

- Investigators usually work for or with the Folly.
- Police investigators and civilian consultants can be on the same team.
- Magic exists but is not public knowledge in normal society.
- Some investigators may know Newtonian magic; others contribute through mundane expertise.
- The game supports investigative, social, magical, and physical problem-solving.
